\noindent\textbf{Data.}
We evaluate 39 RGB-D loop closures from 18 Kimera-Multi
sequences~\cite{tian23arxiv_kimeramultiexperiments} and 12 LiDAR cases from
six KITTI sequences~\cite{Geiger2013IJRR}. Each sequence is processed once
end-to-end: ORB-SLAM3 and Voxblox run from the start, keyframe range images
are cached online (0.9 -- 3.6\,GiB of 16-bit RGB-D, 1349 -- 5707
keyframes), and each WarpVox correction replaces the active volume before
fusion resumes.

\noindent\textbf{Reference and alignment.}
All methods reconstruct the same interval. The reference is the official
Kimera-Multi ground-truth cloud within a 5\,m tube around the GT trajectory,
matching Voxblox's integration range, or the KITTI Velodyne cloud aggregated
at GT poses within the 25\,m ray range. Predictions are aligned by
timestamp-matched trajectory positions only: one alignment per case for all
reconstructions driven by the corrected ORB-SLAM3~\cite{ORBSLAM3_TRO}
trajectory, and its own optimized trajectory for Voxgraph.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
Uncorrected Voxblox keeps the pre-correction trajectory.
Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3dmodernlibrary3d} (TSDF),
Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} (wavelet occupancy), and Voxblox
reintegration~\cite{Oleynikova_2017}, the representation-matched reference,
re-fuse every range frame with the corrected trajectory;
Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph} optimizes rigid TSDF submaps. WarpVox
starts from the active uncorrected TSDF and uses keyframe measurements only
for its targets.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and timing.}
F@10/25/50 is $100\cdot2PR/(P+R)$, with $P$ and $R$ the fractions of
predicted and reference points within 0.10/0.25/0.50\,m of the other
surface, and CD is the symmetric mean nearest-neighbour distance; all
methods share each case's crop, two-million-point sample, and seed. TSDFs
use 0.25\,m voxels and 1.0\,m truncation, and Wavemap a matched 0.25\,m
finest level evaluated on its occupancy point cloud. The 11 cases at
0.10\,m and the ten Kimera-Multi sequences used for the PGMO comparison and
ablation were sampled once. Times are in-memory core map updates on an AMD
Ryzen 7 9700X with 64\,GB RAM, excluding disk I/O and PGMO MeshFrontend
construction.

\subsection{Reconstruction Fidelity and Update Cost}

At 0.25\,m, WarpVox stays within 0.32\,pp of reintegration at F@25 and
within 1.5\,cm in CD on dataset means, below every other correction baseline
in CD, while updating the map $48.43\times$ faster on Kimera-Multi and
$3.23\times$ faster on KITTI (Table~\ref{tab:broad_main}). Per-event F@25
differences range from $-2.91$ to $+2.59$\,pp on Kimera-Multi and from
$-3.03$ to $+1.80$\,pp on KITTI, around means of $+0.05$ and $-0.32$\,pp.

Stage times follow their workloads (Table~\ref{tab:runtime_breakdown}):
association grows with the candidate vertex -- keyframe pairs, as RGB-D
queries every source vertex and LiDAR one vertex per occupied 0.5\,m cell;
target construction grows with the retained observations, and deformation
and reconstruction with surface and volume size. The smaller KITTI speedup
reflects lower-cost sparse-LiDAR replay and WarpVox's larger active surface
and output volume there.
Voxgraph improves over uncorrected Voxblox at every threshold by aligning
rigid SDF submaps, but stays $5.98$ and $12.08$\,pp below WarpVox at F@25 on
the two datasets, consistent with uncorrected intra-submap drift. Wavemap
shows low F@10 but competitive KITTI F@50, consistent with a hierarchical
occupancy representation whose 0.25\,m setting is its finest level; its
grid-centred evaluation points can sit up to 0.22\,m from the true surface.
At 0.10\,m on 11 Kimera-Multi cases, WarpVox reaches 17.13/39.77/57.83 at
F@10/25/50 against 17.21/39.68/57.12 for reintegration, $20.91\times$ faster
(9.207 vs.\ 192.548\,s). Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_ral} compares the maps.

\subsection{Comparison with Surface-Deformation Methods}
\label{sec:pgmo}

A correction combines a back-end trajectory with the surface targets built
on it. Kimera-PGMO shares WarpVox's input and isolates the
correction method; ElasticFusion computes its own trajectory and isolates
its source.

\noindent\textbf{Kimera-PGMO.}
Both methods use the same timestamped $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ Kimera-VIO pre-mesh
and the VIO pre-correction and PGMO-optimized trajectories; PGMO runs its
official
\texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak deformer}~\cite{kimera_pgmo}
in one-shot mode, the stronger of its two modes (183.8\,cm CD online), and
WarpVox runs Stages~1 -- 3. With the input fixed, WarpVox improves F@25/F@50
by $0.26/0.91$\,pp and lowers CD from 179.0 to 152.9\,cm in $2.34\times$ less
time, within $0.20$\,pp at F@10, which approaches the
$8\,\mathrm{cm}$ mesh resolution (Table~\ref{tab:pgmo_full10}). Its complete
update with an $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ native TSDF, $4.792\,\mathrm{s}$, is faster
than PGMO's surface correction alone and reaches F@25/F@50 of
$24.387/43.098\%$.

\noindent\textbf{ElasticFusion.}
ElasticFusion~\cite{whelan2016elasticfusion} corrects its map only through
the registrations of its own tracking. We ran it as in its published
evaluation on TUM fr3/long\_office\_household~\cite{sturm2012tum}, its own
benchmark, and on the ten Kimera-Multi sequences at their last evaluated
loop event. On TUM it tracks as published (1.80\,cm ATE RMSE against the
reported 1.7\,cm); against a reference fused from the RGB-D measurements at
motion-capture poses, WarpVox leads at every threshold, with F@1/2/5 of
$37.61/72.13/89.53\%$ and 2.82\,cm CD against $33.17/62.77/83.44\%$ and
3.69\,cm for ElasticFusion and $33.23/68.64/89.04\%$ and 2.99\,cm for
reintegration. On Kimera-Multi, ElasticFusion's trajectory drifts by a mean
ATE of 115.8\,m and its F@25 falls to 1.98\%, while WarpVox stays within
0.61\,pp of reintegration (23.68 vs.\ 24.29\%). WarpVox applies the
corrections of the back-end the system uses, ORB-SLAM3 and Kimera-PGMO here,
to RGB-D and LiDAR maps alike, so the map follows the back-end best suited to
the data.

Nearest-KF moves each control by its closest keyframe's pose correction.
The observation-derived targets gain 0.637\,pp at F@25 over this baseline,
and the gain comes from the full backward construction of Stages~1 -- 2:
recovering, screening, weighting, and combining the measurements of several
keyframes. A matched multi-keyframe pose transport that keeps Stages~1 -- 2
and replaces only the measured samples of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corrected_target}
performs on par ($+0.042$\,pp, positive in 8 of 10 cases, $-0.04$ to
$+0.09$\,pp), since the two candidates differ by the association residual,
which the Stage-1 gate bounds within one voxel; removing the gate and
reliability screening drops F@25 to 25.49\%, below nearest-keyframe
transport. One-at-a-time changes of the association, trimming, and Stage-3
thresholds move mean F@25 by $-0.19$ to $+0.05$\,pp.
The robust 60K QEM proxy stays within 0.032\,pp of full-mesh ARAP at F@25
while cutting Stage~3 time from 3.323 to 1.408\,s. Stage~4 keeps the
re-extracted mesh within 0.122\,pp of the robust Stage~3 surface and
improves F@25 by 0.216\,pp over mesh-only conversion for 0.062\,s more.
Reintegrating only the cached keyframes reaches $24.18/41.62$ at F@25/F@50
in $33.70$\,s, $2.44/3.78$\,pp below WarpVox: the cache supports target
construction but not a complete volumetric rebuild.

\subsection{Repeated Correction and Continued Fusion}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/cycles.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Repeated correction and continued fusion on five
multi-loop sequences.} $\Delta$F against reintegration and the four
volumetric metrics after each correction (A) and after continued fusion (B).}
\label{fig:cycles}
\end{figure*}

We follow the five multi-loop sequences of the ablation set, with two to six
loop closures each and 15 corrections in total, and record checkpoints after
each correction (A) and after continued fusion up to the next loop closure
(B, ten intervals of 1.44 -- 599.03\,s; Fig.~\ref{fig:cycles}). The
reintegration reference uses the same measurement history and corrected
poses. Against the ground truth, WarpVox stays close to reintegration, with
mean $\Delta$F@25 of $+0.36$\,pp after correction and $-0.01$\,pp after
continued fusion, rising in some sequences and falling in others with no
common trend.

The volumes are compared with reintegration as a pseudo-ground-truth
reference on a shared grid, with unallocated voxels at zero weight:
positive-weight support IoU, weighted Jaccard similarity of voxel weights,
reference-weighted free-space recall for $\Phi_{\rm r}>0.5s$, and TSDF-sign
accuracy on common support outside the $0.5s$ zero-level band. Their
differences come from reconstructing each corrected TSDF from its mesh, an
approximation each correction repeats; continued fusion with the same
Voxblox update raises all four metrics in every interval and restores the
agreement where later frames revisit the map. Successive corrections mainly
narrow the shared domain measured by support IoU and weighted Jaccard, while
sign accuracy and free-space recall stay close to reintegration within it
(92.2\% and 97.3\% after correction), so most of the volume remains usable.

\subsection{Cross-Representation Case Study}

The targets come from range measurements and transfer beyond TSDFs. On one
Kimera-Multi sequence, displacing only the Gaussian centres of a
SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam} reconstruction based on 3D Gaussian
Splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} by the resulting ARAP deformation, with
colours, opacities, scales, and covariances fixed, reaches 23.12/40.59\% at
F@25/F@50 and 142.54\,cm depth L1 in 21\,s, close to a SplaTAM rebuild with
the corrected trajectory (23.25/41.01\%, 141.37\,cm, about 2\,h) and up from
17.23/35.25\% and 173.93\,cm before correction. PSNR and MS-SSIM rise from
11.96 to 12.59 and from 0.512 to 0.526, and LPIPS falls from 0.430 to 0.411.
This single-sequence case study tests target transferability only;
WarpVox's implementation and primary evaluation target native TSDF
correction for continued robotic mapping.
