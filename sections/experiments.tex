Each bag is processed once end-to-end; individual loop-closure checkpoints are not replayed independently. ORB-SLAM3 and Voxblox run from the start of the bag; at each trajectory revision, WarpVox corrects the current active TSDF and commits the returned native volume back to Voxblox, which continues fusion from that state until the next loop closure. We evaluate 39 RGB-D loop-closure cases from 18 Kimera-Multi sequences~\cite{tian23arxiv_kimeramultiexperiments} and 12 LiDAR cases from six KITTI sequences~\cite{Geiger2013IJRR}. 
% Each bag is run through ORB-SLAM3 once, and all loop-closure events it produces are recorded. 
Every method reconstructs the same temporal interval. For Kimera-Multi, the reference geometry is the official ground-truth point cloud, cropped to a 5 m tube around the GT trajectory to match Voxblox's maximum integration range. 
For KITTI, the reference is the Velodyne point cloud aggregated at ground-truth poses, within the mapper's 25 m ray range. 
Predictions expressed outside the GT frame are aligned to it using only timestamp-matched trajectory positions; no mesh-to-GT registration is used. Voxgraph is aligned using the trajectory recovered from its jointly optimized submap-pose graph, whereas reconstructions built from the same corrected ORB-SLAM3 trajectory share one alignment per case.

WarpVox runs alongside the standard Voxblox fusion pipeline and automatically caches the modality-native range image of each required SLAM keyframe as it arrives. Thus, no bag rewind or separate keyframe-image preprocessing is required at loop events. Event-specific associations, targets, and deformation state are discarded after each committed correction, whereas the keyframe cache and corrected active TSDF persist across events. For the eighteen RGB-D sequences, the cache holds 0.9--3.6\,GiB of uncompressed 16-bit keyframe depth images (mean 2.1 GiB over 1349–5707 keyframes).


\noindent\textbf{Baselines.} Uncorrected Voxblox TSDF uses the pre-correction trajectory. Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3dmodernlibrary3d}, a widely used third-party library, provides an independent corrected-pose TSDF rebuild; Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} represents efficient occupancy mapping with a wavelet occupancy rebuild, and Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, the submap-based online correction baseline, optimizes rigid TSDF submaps. Voxblox reintegration~\cite{Oleynikova_2017} rebuilds the same TSDF representation from the corrected trajectory and is the representation-matched reference. WarpVox starts from the active uncorrected TSDF. At each loop event, WarpVox, Voxblox reintegration, Open3D, and Wavemap receive the same corrected keyframe trajectory; Voxgraph retains its independently optimized submap frame as described above. Voxblox reintegration, Open3D, and Wavemap re-fuse every range frame, interpolating the corrected trajectory to each frame timestamp; WarpVox uses only the keyframe measurements, and only to construct targets. We compute precision and recall by bidirectional nearest-neighbour tests and report F@10/25/50 at 0.10/0.25/0.50 m. All methods share each case's crop, spatial region, two-million-point sample, random seed, and thresholds. Main TSDF experiments use 0.25 m voxels and 1.0 m truncation; Wavemap uses a matched 0.25 m finest level. Lacking a surface extractor, Wavemap is evaluated on its official occupancy point cloud, one point per occupied cell. The 11-case high-resolution subset and ten-bag subset were each randomly selected once; the latter is shared by the PGMO comparison and ablation.

\noindent\textbf{Timing protocol.} We report core map-update time from in-memory state on an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X with 64\,GB RAM: TSDF/occupancy integration, submap or mesh optimization, or WarpVox source-mesh-to-TSDF correction, as applicable. Disk I/O and processing outside correction, including PGMO MeshFrontend construction, are excluded.

\subsection{Reconstruction Fidelity and Update Cost}

Table~\ref{tab:broad_main} gives the uniform 0.25 m comparison. WarpVox is the closest method to Voxblox reintegration at every threshold.



On Kimera-Multi, WarpVox differs from reintegration by $+0.20/+0.05/-0.28$ pp at F@10/F@25/F@50. These sub-percentage-point differences indicate close agreement in the reported mean F-scores. WarpVox is $48.43\times$ faster than reintegration on Kimera-Multi and $3.23\times$ on KITTI. The smaller KITTI gain is consistent with cheap sparse-LiDAR replay and WarpVox's larger active surface and output volume. Across multi-loop sequences, the mean first-to-last change in the WarpVox--reintegration F@25 gap remains within one percentage point. Voxgraph improves over Voxblox$_{\rm Pre}$ at every threshold by aligning rigid SDF submaps, but stays $5.98$ and $12.08$ pp below WarpVox at F@25 on the two datasets, consistent with uncorrected intra-submap drift. Wavemap's profile---low F@10 but competitive KITTI F@50---is consistent with a hierarchical occupancy representation whose 0.25 m setting is its finest level, not a uniform map resolution; its grid-centred evaluation points can also sit up to 0.22\,m from the true surface.

\subsection{Comparison with Kimera-PGMO}
\label{sec:pgmo}

Both methods use the Kimera-VIO trajectory and corresponding $8\,\text{cm}$ frontend mesh produced by the same run. For each bag, the PGMO mesh is scored directly, while its corresponding optimized trajectory defines the VIO-to-PGMO correction applied by WarpVox to the VIO pre-mesh. Kimera-PGMO's cost is necessarily cumulative---its final mesh is the product of incremental deformation-graph optimization---whereas WarpVox corrects the corresponding VIO pre-mesh in one pass. Because this pre-mesh carries no source TSDF, WarpVox uses mesh-only Stage~4; this experiment compares surface correction without source-volume support transfer.

Observation-derived targets recover a more accurate surface than naive nearest-keyframe transport---which moves each retained control using its spatially closest keyframe's pose correction---improving all three mean F-scores, including 0.637 pp at F@25. The robust 60K QEM proxy remains within 0.032 pp of full-mesh ARAP at F@25 while reducing Stage~3 time from 3.323 to 1.408 s. The complete source-supported reconstruction keeps the re-extracted mesh within 0.122 pp of the robust Stage-3 surface. Source-supported reconstruction further raises mean F@25 by 0.216 pp over mesh-only conversion at an additional 0.062 s. 

Beyond the stage ablation, reintegrating only the cached keyframes on the same ten cases reaches $24.18/41.62$ at F@25/F@50 in $33.70$\,s, still an order of magnitude above WarpVox and $2.44/3.78$ pp below it: the cache supports target construction but not a complete volumetric rebuild.

\noindent\textbf{TSDF consistency.} Support IoU compares the positive-weight voxel sets, while weight-mass IoU replaces set intersection and union by $\sum_x\min(w^+(x),w_{\rm r}(x))$ and $\sum_x\max(w^+(x),w_{\rm r}(x))$, respectively. Free-space coverage is weighted by $w_{\rm r}$ over reference voxels with $\Phi_{\rm r}>0.5s$, and sign agreement is evaluated on common support outside $|\Phi_{\rm r}|<0.5s$. On ten $0.25$-m cases, WarpVox attains $86.19\%$ support IoU and $80.06\%$ weight-mass IoU with corrected-pose Voxblox reintegration. It preserves $98.50\%$ of the reference-weighted free-space support, while TSDF signs agree for $96.29\%$ of common supported voxels outside the zero-level ambiguity band. These results show that WarpVox closely matches reintegration in observed support, accumulated weight, and free/occupied structure.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-representation 3DGS~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} case study.} Applying the same corrected surface targets to the Gaussian centres of one Kimera-Multi sequence reconstructed with SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam} improves F@25/F@50 from 17.23/35.25 to 23.12/40.59 and depth L1 from 173.93 to 142.54\,cm in 21\,s; a corrected-pose rebuild obtains 23.25/41.01 and 141.37\,cm in approximately 2\,h. This single-sequence result suggests the target construction can transfer beyond voxel grids; WarpVox itself remains targeted at TSDF mapping pipelines.
