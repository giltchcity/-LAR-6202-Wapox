\noindent\textbf{Data and execution.}
Each bag is processed once end-to-end; loop-closure checkpoints are not
replayed. ORB-SLAM3 and Voxblox run from the start, and each WarpVox
correction replaces the active volume before fusion resumes. Keyframe range
images are cached online, eliminating bag rewind and separate preprocessing
at loop events. Associations, targets, and deformation state are discarded
after each correction, while the cache and corrected TSDF persist. We
evaluate 39 RGB-D loop closures from 18 Kimera-Multi
sequences~\cite{tian23arxiv_kimeramultiexperiments} and 12 LiDAR cases from
six KITTI sequences~\cite{Geiger2013IJRR}. The uncompressed 16-bit RGB-D
cache occupies 0.9 -- 3.6\,GiB (mean 2.1\,GiB; 1349 -- 5707 keyframes).

\noindent\textbf{Reference and alignment.}
All methods reconstruct the same temporal interval. Kimera-Multi uses the
official ground-truth point cloud cropped to a 5\,m tube around the GT
trajectory, matching Voxblox's maximum integration range. KITTI uses the
Velodyne cloud aggregated at ground-truth poses within the mapper's 25\,m
ray range. Predictions outside the GT frame are aligned using only
timestamp-matched trajectory positions; no mesh-to-GT registration is used.
Voxgraph uses the trajectory recovered from its optimized submap-pose graph,
while reconstructions driven by the corrected ORB-SLAM3~\cite{ORBSLAM3_TRO} trajectory share one
alignment per case.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
Uncorrected Voxblox uses the pre-correction trajectory.
Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3dmodernlibrary3d} provides an independent TSDF
rebuild, Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} a wavelet occupancy rebuild,
and Voxblox reintegration~\cite{Oleynikova_2017} the representation-matched
TSDF reference. These methods re-fuse every range frame using the corrected
trajectory interpolated to each timestamp. Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}
optimizes rigid TSDF submaps in its independently optimized frame. WarpVox
starts from the active uncorrected TSDF and uses keyframe measurements only
for target construction.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
We report F@10/25/50 from bidirectional nearest-neighbour tests at
0.10/0.25/0.50\,m. We also report the symmetric Chamfer distance (CD),
the mean nearest-neighbour distance in both directions. All methods share each case's crop, two-million-point
sample, random seed, and thresholds. Main TSDF experiments use 0.25\,m
voxels and 1.0\,m truncation; Wavemap uses a matched 0.25\,m finest level
and is evaluated on its official occupancy point cloud. The 11-case
high-resolution subset and ten-bag subset were each sampled once; the latter
is shared by the PGMO comparison and ablation.

\noindent\textbf{Timing.}
We report in-memory core map-update time on an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X with
64\,GB RAM, including TSDF or occupancy integration, submap or mesh
optimization, or the applicable WarpVox stages; disk I/O and PGMO
MeshFrontend construction are excluded.

On Kimera-Multi, WarpVox differs from reintegration by
$+0.20/+0.05/-0.28$ pp at F@10/F@25/F@50, and on KITTI by $-0.32$\,pp at
F@25 in dataset mean. Its CD stays within 1.5\,cm of reintegration on both
datasets, below every other correction baseline. Per-event F@25 differences fall on both sides of zero, from
$-2.91$ to $+2.59$\,pp and from $-3.03$ to $+1.80$\,pp. WarpVox is $48.43\times$ faster
than reintegration on Kimera-Multi and $3.23\times$ on KITTI. The KITTI speedup reflects lower-cost sparse-LiDAR replay and WarpVox's
larger active surface and output volume.
Stage shares follow the same workloads
(Table~\ref{tab:runtime_breakdown}): LiDAR association searches
spherical-image neighbourhoods, and RGB-D target construction aggregates more
retained observations. Voxgraph improves over uncorrected
Voxblox at every threshold by aligning rigid SDF submaps, but stays $5.98$
and $12.08$ pp below WarpVox at F@25 on the two datasets, consistent with
uncorrected intra-submap drift. Wavemap shows low F@10 but competitive KITTI
F@50. This is consistent with a hierarchical occupancy representation whose
0.25\,m setting is its finest level rather than a uniform map resolution;
its grid-centred evaluation points can also sit up to 0.22\,m from the true
surface.

At 0.10\,m on 11 Kimera-Multi cases, WarpVox obtains 17.13/39.77/57.83 at
F@10/25/50 versus 17.21/39.68/57.12 for reintegration, while reducing core
time from 192.548 to 9.207\,s ($20.91\times$).
Table~\ref{tab:runtime_breakdown} separates association, target estimation,
deformation, topology processing, and TSDF reconstruction at both
resolutions. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_ral} complements the quantitative
comparison.

\subsection{Comparison with Kimera-PGMO}
\label{sec:pgmo}

This experiment compares Kimera-PGMO's deformation-graph correction with
WarpVox's observation-derived targets and proxy ARAP. Both use the same
timestamped $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ Kimera-VIO pre-mesh and the same VIO
pre-correction and PGMO-optimized trajectories; PGMO uses the official
\texttt{mesh\_trajectory\_deformer} tool~\cite{kimera_pgmo}, whereas
WarpVox applies Stages~1 -- 3.

At F@10, which approaches the $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ frontend-mesh resolution,
WarpVox remains within $0.20$~pp of Kimera-PGMO. It improves F@25/F@50 by
$0.26/0.91$~pp while reducing corrected-surface core time by $2.34\times$. It
also lowers CD from 179.0\,cm for one-shot PGMO, the stronger of its two
modes (183.8\,cm online), to 152.9\,cm.
WarpVox subsequently applies Stage~4 to reconstruct an $8\,\mathrm{cm}$
native TSDF, adding $1.277\,\mathrm{s}$ ($4.792\,\mathrm{s}$ for
Stages~1 -- 4) and yielding F@25/F@50 scores of $24.387\%/43.098\%$. This
complete update with a native TSDF stays faster than PGMO's surface
correction alone (8.231\,s).

\noindent\textbf{ElasticFusion.}
ElasticFusion~\cite{whelan2016elasticfusion}, which also deforms its map
through an embedded deformation graph, derives the correction from its own
tracking and loop closure. On TUM
fr3/long\_office\_household~\cite{sturm2012tum}, its own benchmark, where it
tracks as published (1.80\,cm ATE), WarpVox leads it at every F-score
threshold and in CD (2.82 vs.\ 3.69\,cm); on the ten Kimera-Multi sequences
its tracking drifts by 115.8\,m ATE, while WarpVox, applying the system's
back-end correction, stays within 0.61\,pp of reintegration at F@25.

Nearest-KF moves each control by its closest keyframe's pose correction.
Observation-derived targets improve all three F-scores over this baseline,
including 0.637 pp at F@25. The gain comes from Stages~1 -- 2 as a whole, which
recover, screen, weight, and combine the measurements of several keyframes:
a matched multi-keyframe pose transport that keeps both stages and replaces
only the measured samples of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corrected_target} performs on par
($+0.042$\,pp, positive in 8 of 10 cases), as the two candidates differ by
the association residual that the Stage-1 gate bounds within one voxel,
whereas removing the gate and reliability screening drops F@25 to 25.49\%,
below nearest-keyframe transport. The robust 60K QEM proxy remains within 0.032 pp
of full-mesh ARAP at F@25 while reducing Stage~3 time from 3.323 to
1.408\,s. Stage~4 keeps the re-extracted mesh within 0.122 pp of the robust
Stage~3 surface and improves F@25 by 0.216 pp over mesh-only conversion, at
an additional cost of 0.062\,s. Varying the association, trimming, and Stage-3
thresholds one at a time changes mean F@25 by $-0.19$ to $+0.05$\,pp.

Beyond the stage ablation, reintegrating only the cached keyframes on the
same ten cases reaches $24.18/41.62$ at F@25/F@50 in $33.70$\,s and still
takes over an order of magnitude longer than WarpVox, while remaining
$2.44/3.78$ pp below it. The cache therefore supports target construction
but not a complete volumetric rebuild.

\noindent\textbf{TSDF consistency.}
We compare WarpVox with corrected-pose Voxblox reintegration on a shared
voxel grid, treating unallocated voxels as zero weight. We report
positive-weight support IoU, weighted Jaccard similarity of voxel weights,
reference-weighted free-space support recall for $\Phi_{\rm r}>0.5s$, and
TSDF-sign accuracy on common support outside the $0.5s$ zero-level band.
Averaged over ten $0.25$-m checkpoints, WarpVox achieves 86.19\%, 80.06\%,
98.50\%, and 96.29\%, respectively, showing substantial agreement in
support, accumulated weight, and TSDF sign structure.

\noindent\textbf{Repeated correction and continued fusion.}
The five multi-loop sequences of the ten bags, with two to six loop closures
each and 15 corrections in total, give checkpoints after each correction (A)
and after continued fusion up to the next loop closure (B, ten intervals of
1.44 -- 599.03\,s; Fig.~\ref{fig:cycles}). With no ground-truth TSDF,
reintegration of the same measurement history under the corrected poses is
the pseudo-ground-truth reference for the four metrics, while F-scores stay
referenced to the ground truth. At F@25, WarpVox differs from reintegration
by $+0.50$\,pp after correction and $-0.01$\,pp after continued fusion on
average, with no common trend over successive corrections. Each correction
reconstructs the TSDF from its mesh again, an approximation that mainly
narrows the shared domain measured by support IoU and weighted Jaccard,
while sign accuracy and free-space recall stay close to reintegration within
it (92.2\% and 97.3\% on average after correction). Continued fusion with
the same Voxblox update raises all four metrics in every interval and
restores the agreement where later frames revisit the map.



\noindent\textbf{Cross-representation 3DGS case study.}
Table~\ref{tab:gaussian_case} evaluates whether the observation-derived
correction transfers beyond TSDFs. We apply the same observation-derived
targets to a SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam} reconstruction based on 3D
Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}. This is a geometry-only
diagnostic: each Gaussian centre is displaced by the resulting ARAP
deformation, while colours, opacities, scales, covariances, densification,
pruning, and rendering losses remain fixed. For comparison, we retain the original pre-correction SplaTAM reconstruction
and rebuild SplaTAM from scratch using the corrected trajectory.

The centre-only correction approaches the corrected-pose rebuild at both
surface thresholds and in depth error, while taking 21\,s instead of
approximately 2\,h. With appearance parameters fixed, PSNR and MS-SSIM
increase from 11.96 to 12.59 and from 0.512 to 0.526, while LPIPS decreases
from 0.430 to 0.411. This single-sequence case study tests target
transferability only; WarpVox's implementation and primary evaluation remain
focused on native TSDF correction for continued robotic mapping.
