\noindent\textbf{Data and execution.}
Each bag is processed once end-to-end. ORB-SLAM3 and Voxblox run from the start, and each WarpVox
correction replaces the active volume before fusion resumes. Keyframe range
images are cached online, eliminating bag rewind and preprocessing
at loop events. We
evaluate 39 RGB-D loop closures from 18 Kimera-Multi
sequences~\cite{tian23arxiv_kimeramultiexperiments} and 12 LiDAR cases from
six KITTI sequences~\cite{Geiger2013IJRR}. The PGMO comparison, repeated
correction, and ablation share ten Kimera-Multi sequences, and the 0.10\,m
evaluation uses 11 of the 39 cases; both subsets were sampled once. The uncompressed 16-bit RGB-D
cache occupies 0.9 -- 3.6\,GiB (mean 2.1\,GiB; 1349 -- 5707 keyframes).

\noindent\textbf{Reference and alignment.}
All methods reconstruct the same temporal interval. Kimera-Multi uses the
official ground-truth point cloud cropped to a 5\,m tube around the GT
trajectory, matching Voxblox's maximum integration range. KITTI uses the
Velodyne cloud aggregated at ground-truth poses within the mapper's 25\,m
ray range. Predictions outside the GT frame are aligned using only
timestamp-matched trajectory positions.
Voxgraph uses the trajectory of its optimized submap-pose graph,
while reconstructions driven by the corrected ORB-SLAM3~\cite{ORBSLAM3_TRO} trajectory share one
alignment per case.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
Uncorrected Voxblox uses the pre-correction trajectory.
Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3dmodernlibrary3d} provides an independent TSDF
rebuild, Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} a wavelet occupancy rebuild,
and Voxblox reintegration~\cite{Oleynikova_2017} the representation-matched
TSDF reference. These methods re-fuse every range frame using the corrected
trajectory interpolated to each timestamp. Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}
optimizes rigid TSDF submaps in its independently optimized frame. WarpVox
starts from the active uncorrected TSDF and uses keyframe measurements only
for target construction.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and timing.}
We report F@10/25/50 from bidirectional nearest-neighbour tests at
0.10/0.25/0.50\,m and the symmetric Chamfer distance (CD), the mean
nearest-neighbour distance in both directions. All methods share each case's
crop, two-million-point sample, and random seed. Main TSDF experiments use
0.25\,m voxels and 1.0\,m truncation; Wavemap uses a matched 0.25\,m finest
level and is evaluated on its official occupancy point cloud. Core map-update time is measured in
memory on an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X with 64\,GB RAM and covers integration,
optimization, or the applicable WarpVox stages, excluding disk I/O and PGMO
MeshFrontend construction.

On Kimera-Multi, WarpVox differs from reintegration by
$+0.20/+0.05/-0.28$\,pp at F@10/F@25/F@50, and on KITTI by $-0.32$\,pp at
F@25 in dataset mean. Its CD stays within 1.5\,cm of reintegration on both
datasets, below every other correction baseline. Per-event F@25 differences fall on both sides of zero, from $-2.91$ to $+2.59$\,pp on Kimera-Multi and from $-3.03$ to
$+1.80$\,pp on KITTI. WarpVox is $48.43\times$ faster
than reintegration on Kimera-Multi and $3.23\times$ on KITTI. The KITTI speedup reflects lower-cost sparse-LiDAR replay and WarpVox's
larger active surface and output volume. Voxgraph improves over uncorrected
Voxblox at every threshold by aligning rigid SDF submaps, but stays $5.98$
and $12.08$\,pp below WarpVox at F@25 on the two datasets, consistent with
uncorrected intra-submap drift. Wavemap shows low F@10 but competitive KITTI
F@50. This is consistent with a hierarchical occupancy representation whose
0.25\,m setting is its finest level rather than a uniform map resolution;
its grid-centred evaluation points can also sit up to 0.22\,m from the true
surface.

At 0.10\,m on 11 Kimera-Multi cases, WarpVox obtains 17.13/39.77/57.83 at
F@10/25/50 versus 17.21/39.68/57.12 for reintegration, while reducing core
time from 192.548 to 9.207\,s ($20.91\times$).
Table~\ref{tab:runtime_breakdown} separates association, target estimation,
deformation, topology processing, and TSDF reconstruction at both
resolutions.
Stage shares follow their workloads: LiDAR association searches
spherical-image neighbourhoods, and RGB-D target construction aggregates more
retained observations. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_ral} complements the quantitative
comparison at 0.25\,m.

\subsection{Comparison with Deformation-Based Correction}
\label{sec:pgmo}

ElasticFusion~\cite{whelan2016elasticfusion}, a self-contained surfel SLAM
system, deforms its map through an embedded deformation graph derived from
the registrations of its own tracking. Run as in its published evaluation,
it tracks as reported on TUM~\cite{sturm2012tum}, its own room-scale
benchmark (1.80\,cm ATE), with 62.77\% F@2 and 3.69\,cm CD; WarpVox reaches 72.13\% and
2.82\,cm. On the larger Kimera-Multi sequences, its own tracking accumulates
115.8\,m ATE, leaving its map at 1.98\% F@25. WarpVox applies trajectory
corrections to TSDF maps for RGB-D and LiDAR alike, which suits the growing
scale and sensing of today's mapping data.

Kimera-PGMO, like WarpVox, propagates trajectory corrections to a fused map,
and, like ElasticFusion, deforms the surface through an embedded deformation
graph, which ties each mesh vertex to the pose at its timestamp; WarpVox ties
each surface region to the keyframes that observe it. Both correct the same
timestamped $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ Kimera-VIO pre-mesh with the same VIO
pre-correction and PGMO-optimized trajectories, PGMO through its official
\texttt{mesh\_trajectory\_deformer} tool~\cite{kimera_pgmo} and WarpVox
through Stages~1 -- 3, isolating the correction method.

\subsection{TSDF Consistency over Repeated Correction}
\label{sec:consistency}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figures/cycles.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Repeated correction (A, 15) and continued fusion (B, 10).}}
\label{fig:cycles}
\end{figure}

We follow the five multi-loop sequences of the ten through repeated
correction and continued fusion, with checkpoints after each correction (A) and after
continued fusion up to the next loop closure (B; Fig.~\ref{fig:cycles}).
F-scores compare each map with the ground truth. The volumetric metrics
compare it with Voxblox reintegration of the same measurement history, as the
reference since no ground-truth TSDF exists. On a shared grid with
unallocated voxels at zero weight, they are support IoU over positive-weight
voxels, weighted Jaccard similarity $\sum\min(w,w_{\rm r})/\sum\max(w,w_{\rm r})$,
$w_{\rm r}$-weighted free-space recall over voxels with
${\Phi_{\rm r}>0.5s}$, and sign accuracy on common support outside the
$0.5s$ zero-level band.

At F@25, WarpVox differs from reintegration by $+0.50$\,pp after correction
and $-0.01$\,pp after continued fusion on average, with per-event differences
from $-1.48$ to $+2.55$\,pp after correction. Over the 15 post-correction
checkpoints, it averages 83.4\%, 80.1\%, 97.3\%, and 92.2\% on the four
volumetric metrics. Rebuilding the TSDF from the corrected mesh at each
correction mainly narrows the shared support measured by IoU and weighted
Jaccard, while free-space recall and sign accuracy remain high within it.
Continued fusion with the same Voxblox update raises all four metrics in
every interval, restoring agreement in revisited regions.

Nearest-KF moves each control by its closest keyframe's pose correction.
Observation-derived targets aim to reverse fusion: they recover the keyframe
measurements that formed the source TSDF at each vertex, then screen, weight,
and combine them (Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}). They improve all three F-scores over
this baseline, including 0.637\,pp at F@25. Replacing only
their measured samples of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corrected_target} with each keyframe's
pose transport performs on par, with WarpVox $0.042$\,pp ahead at F@25 on
average and in 8 of 10 cases ($-0.04$ to $+0.09$\,pp), while removing the gate
and reliability screening drops F@10/25/50 to 8.93/25.49/45.29\%, below
nearest-keyframe transport at F@25. The robust 60K QEM proxy remains within 0.032\,pp
of full-mesh ARAP at F@25 while reducing Stage~3 time from 3.323 to
1.408\,s. Stage~4 keeps the re-extracted mesh within 0.122\,pp of the robust
Stage~3 surface and improves F@25 by 0.216\,pp over mesh-only conversion, at
an additional cost of 0.062\,s. Varying the association, trimming, and Stage-3
thresholds one at a time changes mean F@25 by $-0.19$ to $+0.05$\,pp.

Beyond the stage ablation, reintegrating only the cached keyframes on the
same ten cases reaches $24.18/41.62$ at F@25/F@50 in $33.70$\,s, over an order
of magnitude longer than WarpVox, while remaining
$2.44/3.78$\,pp below it.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-representation 3DGS case study.}
Table~\ref{tab:gaussian_case} evaluates whether the observation-derived
correction transfers beyond TSDFs. We apply the same observation-derived
targets to a SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam} reconstruction based on 3D
Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}. This is a geometry-only
diagnostic: each Gaussian centre is displaced by the resulting ARAP
deformation, while all appearance and optimization parameters remain fixed.

The centre-only correction approaches the corrected-pose rebuild at both
surface thresholds and in depth error, while taking 21\,s instead of
approximately 2\,h. PSNR and MS-SSIM
increase from 11.96 to 12.59 and from 0.512 to 0.526, while LPIPS decreases
from 0.430 to 0.411. The same targets thus also correct a
Gaussian map.
