\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}



Each bag is processed once end-to-end; individual loop-closure checkpoints are not replayed independently. ORB-SLAM3 and Voxblox run from the start of the bag; at each trajectory revision, WarpVox corrects the current active TSDF and commits the returned native volume back to Voxblox, which continues fusion from that state until the next loop closure. We evaluate 39 RGB-D loop-closure cases from 18 Kimera-Multi sequences~\cite{tian23arxiv_kimeramultiexperiments} and 12 LiDAR cases from six KITTI sequences~\cite{Geiger2013IJRR}. 
% Each bag is run through ORB-SLAM3 once, and all loop-closure events it produces are recorded. 
Every method reconstructs the same temporal interval. For Kimera-Multi, the reference geometry is the official ground-truth point cloud, cropped to a 5 m tube around the GT trajectory to match Voxblox's maximum integration range. 
For KITTI, the reference is the GT-registered point cloud, within the mapper's 25 m ray range. 
Predictions expressed outside the GT frame are aligned to it using only timestamp-matched trajectory positions; no mesh-to-GT registration is used. Voxgraph is aligned using the trajectory recovered from its jointly optimized submap-pose graph, whereas reconstructions built from the same corrected ORB-SLAM3 trajectory share one alignment per case.

WarpVox runs alongside the standard Voxblox fusion pipeline and automatically caches the modality-native range image of each required SLAM keyframe as it arrives. Thus, no bag rewind or separate keyframe-image preprocessing is required at loop events. Event-specific associations, targets, and deformation state are discarded after each committed correction, whereas the keyframe cache and corrected active TSDF persist across events. For the eighteen RGB-D sequences, the cache holds one uncompressed 16-bit depth image per keyframe at $0.646\,\mathrm{MiB}$, giving $0.9$--$3.6\,\mathrm{GiB}$ over 1349--5707 keyframes (mean $2.1\,\mathrm{GiB}$). 


\noindent\textbf{Baselines.} Uncorrected Voxblox TSDF uses the pre-correction trajectory. Open3D~\cite{zhou2018open3dmodernlibrary3d} is an independent corrected-pose TSDF rebuild; Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} rebuilds a wavelet occupancy map, and Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph} optimizes rigid TSDF submaps. Voxblox reintegration~\cite{Oleynikova_2017} is the representation-matched reference. WarpVox starts from the active uncorrected TSDF. At each loop event, WarpVox, Voxblox reintegration, Open3D, and Wavemap receive the same corrected keyframe trajectory; Voxgraph retains its independently optimized submap frame as described above. For the representation-matched reference, Voxblox interpolates this trajectory to every range-frame timestamp in the reconstructed interval and re-fuses the complete range stream. WarpVox uses the keyframe range measurements only to construct correction targets. We compute precision and recall by bidirectional nearest-neighbour tests and report F@10/25/50 at 0.10/0.25/0.50 m. All methods share each case's crop, spatial region, two-million-point sample, random seed, and thresholds. Main TSDF experiments use 0.25 m voxels and 1.0 m truncation; Wavemap uses a matched 0.25 m finest level. The 11-case high-resolution subset and ten-bag subset were each randomly selected once; the latter is shared by the PGMO comparison and ablation.

\noindent\textbf{Timing protocol.}
For all methods, we report core map-update time from in-memory state on an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X with 64\,GB RAM: TSDF/occupancy integration, submap or mesh optimization, or WarpVox source-mesh-to-TSDF correction, as applicable. Disk I/O, and processing outside correction, including PGMO MeshFrontend construction, are excluded.

\subsection{Comparison with Kimera-PGMO}
\label{sec:pgmo}

Both methods use the Kimera-VIO trajectory and corresponding $5\,\text{cm}$ frontend
mesh produced by the same run. For each bag, the PGMO mesh is scored directly,
while its corresponding optimized trajectory defines the VIO-to-PGMO
correction applied by WarpVox to the VIO pre-mesh. Because this pre-mesh
carries no source TSDF, WarpVox uses mesh-only Stage~4; this experiment
compares surface correction without source-volume support transfer.

Observation-derived targets recover a more accurate surface than naive nearest-keyframe transport---which moves each retained control using its spatially closest keyframe's pose correction---improving all three mean F-scores, including 0.637 pp at F@25. Converting the resulting Stage~3 surface back to a fusible TSDF changes the three mean F-scores by at most 0.122 pp. Source-supported reconstruction further raises mean F@25 by 0.216 pp over mesh-only conversion at an additional 0.062 s.

\noindent\textbf{Stage-4 TSDF sign and weight consistency.} Beyond restoring the corrected zero set, Stage~4 seeks to reconstruct the surrounding volumetric state using the sign and integration weights transferred from the source TSDF. We compare these fields with corrected-pose Voxblox reintegration on the same ten cases. Over voxels with positive weight in both volumes, the TSDF sign agrees for $95.17\%$ of voxels, indicating that the free-space side of the reconstructed surface is largely consistent with reintegration. The integration-weight fields attain a mean weighted Jaccard similarity of $78.52\%$, computed as $\sum_x \min(w^+(x), w_{\rm reint}(x)) / \sum_x \max(w^+(x), w_{\rm reint}(x))$. These results show that Stage~4 reconstructs a corrected native TSDF whose source-derived volumetric fields largely agree with those produced by reintegration.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-representation 3DGS case study.}
To test whether the observation-derived target mechanism transfers beyond TSDFs, we apply the same corrected surface targets to the Gaussian centres of one Kimera-Multi sequence reconstructed with SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam}, while leaving all other Gaussian attributes unchanged. F@25/F@50 improves from 17.23/35.25 to 23.12/40.59, and depth L1 decreases from 173.93 to 142.54 cm in 21 s. A corrected-pose rebuild obtains 23.25/41.01 and 141.37 cm in approximately 2 h. This single-sequence result suggests that the target construction can transfer beyond voxel grids. It is reported only as a representation-transfer case study: Gaussian splats~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians} do not provide the signed-distance and observed-free-space semantics targeted by WarpVox's primary TSDF mapping setting.
