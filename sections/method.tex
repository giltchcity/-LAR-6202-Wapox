\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
Following the local-consistency assumption of
Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, we assume that TSDF fusion under the
drifted pre-correction trajectory remains locally valid: surface geometry and
connectivity are coherent in local neighborhoods although the map is globally
inconsistent. WarpVox corrects this global inconsistency after loop closure;
severely noisy or topologically corrupted source maps are outside its scope.

\subsection{Problem and Correction Inputs} Let $T^-(t),T^+(t)\in SE(3)$ map the sensor frame at time $t$ to the world frame before and after trajectory correction. The map built with $T^-$ consists of a TSDF $\Phi^-$, its weight field $w^-$, voxel size $s$, and truncation distance $\tau$. During online fusion, Voxblox continuously maintains the Marching-Cubes surface $\mathcal M^-=(V^-,F)$ of the active TSDF~\cite{10.1145/37402.37422}. On a trajectory correction, this mesh becomes the source surface. Let $\mathcal A$ denote the active TSDF cells that contribute triangles to $\mathcal M^-$. WarpVox stores the timestamped, modality-native range images of SLAM keyframes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\rm KF}=\{k_i=(t_i,D_i):i\in{\rm KF}\},
\end{equation}
where ${\rm KF}$ indexes the retained SLAM keyframes and $D_i$ is an RGB-D depth image or a spherical LiDAR range image. Together with $T^-$, these measurements form the observation input to Stage~1. The measurements remain available across correction events. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the four stages.

The observation records and warped mesh are intermediate; WarpVox returns
a native Voxblox volume $(\Phi^+,w^+)$.

\subsection{Stage 1: Correspondence Establishment}

Stage~1 establishes projective correspondences between the current source
surface and the keyframe range measurements under the pre-correction
trajectory. For each queried source vertex $v_j^-$ with normal $n_j^-$, a
spatial index over sensor positions retrieves candidate keyframes that may
observe it.


Let $r$ index a candidate measurement with timestamp $t_r$ and range image
$D_r$. The source vertex is transformed into the corresponding sensor frame
and sampled in the measurement domain:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat v_{rj}^-
&=
\left[
[T^-(t_r)]^{-1}\widetilde v_j^-
\right]_{1:3},\\
m_{rj}
&=
\mathcal S_m\!\left(
D_r,\Pi_m(\hat v_{rj}^-)
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sensor_sample}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi_m$ is the pinhole projection for RGB-D or the spherical projection
for LiDAR. For RGB-D, $\mathcal S_m$ samples the projected depth and
back-projects it to the sensor frame. For LiDAR, it selects the closest valid
return in a local neighbourhood of the projected spherical-image location.

Association quality is evaluated from the discrepancy between the projected
source vertex and the sampled measurement. For RGB-D, this includes the
three-dimensional point discrepancy and the projective depth difference.
For LiDAR, the local spherical-image lookup evaluates radial-range and
three-dimensional consistency. Viewing incidence is evaluated in the
pre-correction world frame from the source normal $n_j^-$ and the
sensor-to-vertex direction.

These cues determine whether a candidate is retained and, when retained,
its reliability $\alpha_{rj}$. The verified tuples
$(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})$ form $\mathcal O_j$, and
$\mathcal O=\{\mathcal O_j\}_j$ is passed to Stage~2.

\subsection{Stage 2: Corrected Surface Targets}

The sensor-frame sample $m_{rj}$ is unchanged by a later revision of the
world-frame trajectory. Let $\widehat T_r^+$ denote the corrected pose
associated with record $r$. RGB-D records are resolved by timestamp, while LiDAR records use
the corresponding scan pose. The corrected candidate is
\begin{equation}
q_{rj}^+
=
\left[
\widehat T_r^+\widetilde m_{rj}
\right]_{1:3}.
\label{eq:corrected_target}
\end{equation}

Let
$\mathcal J_j=\{r:(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})\in\mathcal O_j\}$.
The Stage~1 reliability gives the initial target estimate
\begin{equation}
\bar q_j
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target_initial}
\end{equation}

A median-relative residual threshold selects a consistent subset:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_j
&=
\max\!\left(
\epsilon_m,\,
\kappa\operatorname*{median}_{r\in\mathcal J_j}
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|
\right),\\
I_j
&=
\left\{
r\in\mathcal J_j:
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|\le\theta_j
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:target_inliers}
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon_m$ reflects the modality's sampling resolution and $\kappa$
is shared across modalities. When fewer than three candidates are available,
no trimming is applied. The final observation-derived target is
\begin{equation}
v_j^*
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

Each supported source vertex yields one target displacement
$v_j^*-v_j^-$ for Stage~3.




\subsection{Stage 3: Surface Deformation}

Stage~3 converts the sparse observation-derived targets of Stage~2 into a
coherent, topology-aware surface deformation. It consolidates the control
field within connected components, propagates the resulting motion through
ARAP, and produces paired source and warped surfaces for Stage~4.

Let $d_j=v_j^*-v_j^-$ denote an observation-derived control displacement.
A single component-aware robust pass first consolidates the full-resolution
control field. For each control, an inverse-distance predictor is computed
from neighbouring controls in the same connected component, and a
median -- MAD residual test removes sparse, statistically isolated
displacement outliers. Restricting the comparison by component prevents
spatially adjacent but disconnected surfaces from influencing one another.
A small component retained solely by controls that are subsequently rejected
as outliers is omitted from the paired output surfaces.

Each retained component without a surviving measured control receives up to
three spatially separated synthetic constraints carrying the robust local
motion of nearby accepted controls. These constraints complete the
deformation field over unobserved components and remove the gauge freedom of
disconnected ARAP blocks, while remaining distinct from observation-derived
controls.

For large source meshes, component-aware spatial
clustering~\cite{rossignac1993vertexclustering} constructs a triangular
proxy $\mathcal P^-=(P^-,F_P)$ with a budget of approximately
$60\,\mathrm{K}$ vertices.
One QEM representative~\cite{garland1997qem} is selected per occupied cell,
while different connected components remain separate during clustering.
Let $\pi:V^-\rightarrow P^-$ map each source vertex to its proxy
representative. Controls mapped to proxy vertex $a$ are combined as
\begin{equation}
p_a^{(0)}
=
p_a^-
+
\frac{1}{|\mathcal K_a|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal K_a}d_j,
\qquad
\mathcal K_a=\{j:\pi(j)=a\}.
\label{eq:proxy_targets}
\end{equation}

The proxy targets are further consolidated by a robust local rigid field.
For each controlled proxy vertex, WarpVox fits a local rigid transform to
neighbouring source -- target pairs using Welsch
reweighting~\cite{schoenemann1966procrustes,yao2020welsch}. A measured target
is replaced by its rigid prediction only if it is a gross residual and gross
residuals form a strict majority among the measured targets in its connected
component for which local rigid predictions are available. The first robust
pass removes sparse local outliers before coarsening; this second rule
modifies surviving measured targets only under component-level consensus.
Synthetic targets are regularized by the same local field. Let
$\mathcal C_P$ index the resulting proxy targets $p_a^*$.

WarpVox then propagates the consolidated targets with
ARAP~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007}. Define
$\Delta p_{ab}^{\pm}=p_a^{\pm}-p_b^{\pm}$. The deformation is obtained from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{P^+,\{R_a\}}\quad
&\frac{1}{2}
\sum_{a=1}^{|P^-|}
\sum_{b\in\mathcal N_P(a)}
c_{ab}\\[-1mm]
&\qquad\cdot
\left\|
\Delta p_{ab}^+
-
R_a\Delta p_{ab}^-
\right\|^2,\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&R_a\in SO(3),\\
&p_a^+=p_a^*,
\qquad a\in\mathcal C_P .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:arap}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal N_P(a)$ is the proxy one-ring and $c_{ab}$ are symmetric
cotangent-derived weights~\cite{10.1007/978-3-662-05105-4_2}. The
consolidated targets provide positional constraints, while the ARAP energy
propagates their motion through locally rigid surface deformation. The
constrained system is solved using libigl~\cite{libigl}. The standard
configuration solves this objective on the proxy, while the full-resolution
ablation applies the same objective directly to the full-resolution source
mesh.

Proxy displacements are transferred to the source mesh by topology-local
barycentric interpolation. Let $f_j$ be the closest proxy triangle to
$v_j^-$ within $\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$. With barycentric coordinates
$\lambda_{jb}$ of its closest point,
\begin{equation}
v_j^+
=
v_j^-
+
\sum_{b\in f_j}
\lambda_{jb}(p_b^+-p_b^-),
\qquad
\lambda_{jb}\geq0,
\quad
\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}=1.
\label{eq:proxy_transfer}
\end{equation}
Restricting the interpolation to $\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$ prevents
motion transfer between spatially close but disconnected surfaces.

After interpolation, faces exceeding edge-stretch, area-change, or
aspect-ratio bounds define a repair region. Displacements at incident
vertices are iteratively relaxed toward the mean displacement of their mesh
one-ring, with the surrounding valid displacements held fixed. Faces that
continue to violate the edge-stretch or area-change bounds are omitted from
both paired surfaces. No new triangles or connections are introduced, so
the retained source and warped surfaces share the same indexed face subset:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal M}^-=(\bar V^-,\bar F),
\qquad
\mathcal M^+=(\bar V^+,\bar F),
\qquad
\bar F\subseteq F.
\label{eq:paired_surfaces}
\end{equation}
The shared face indexing provides the source -- warped correspondence used
in Stage~4.




\subsection{Stage 4: Source-Supported TSDF Reconstruction}

Stage~4 reconstructs a native TSDF from the paired source and warped
surfaces produced by Stage~3. The warped surface defines the corrected zero
set, while the source TSDF provides volumetric state that a surface alone
does not encode: observed support, sign orientation, integration weights,
and free space. Distance magnitude is recomputed from the warped surface,
whereas source state is transferred through the paired surface
correspondence.

Let $(\Phi^-,w^-)$ denote the source TSDF. The corrected volume uses the
same voxel size $s$ and truncation distance $\tau$ as the source volume.
We write $\rho=3s/2$ for the near-surface margin. WarpVox first allocates
the blocks containing warped-surface vertices together with one layer of
neighbouring blocks. Surface-local reconstruction is restricted to this
neighbourhood, while transported free space may subsequently allocate
additional blocks.

For each voxel $x$ in the surface-local domain, a closest-triangle
query~\cite{10.1145/2601097.2601199} on $\mathcal M^+$ returns face $f$,
closest point $q^+$, and barycentric coordinates $\beta$. Since the paired
surfaces share face indices, the corresponding source point is
\begin{equation}
q^-=\sum_{k\in f}\beta_k\bar v_k^-.
\end{equation}

Let $R_f^\pm\in SO(3)$ be paired local frames formed from the same indexed
triangle edge and the corresponding source and warped mesh normals. Their
relative rotation defines the geometric pullback, while absolute TSDF sign
orientation is determined separately from the source field. The
local-rigid pullback
\begin{equation}
\Psi_f(x)
=
q^-+R_f^-(R_f^+)^\top(x-q^+)
\label{eq:inverse_map}
\end{equation}
maps an output voxel to its corresponding source neighbourhood while
preserving tangential and normal offsets relative to the paired
triangles~\cite{sumner2004deformationtransfer}.

Let
\begin{equation}
x^-=\Psi_f(x),
\qquad
r(x)=\|x-q^+\|,
\qquad
d^+(x)=\min(\tau,r(x)).
\label{eq:source_query}
\end{equation}
A trilinear source sample $(\widetilde\Phi^-,\widetilde w^-)(x^-)$ is valid
when every source voxel with a nonzero interpolation coefficient has
positive weight; its interpolated weight is then transferred. Otherwise, if
the source voxel containing $x^-$ has positive weight, that weight is
transferred without its distance value. Let $w_{\rm src}^+(x)$ denote the
transferred weight. If neither form of source support is available, $x$ is
left to the fallback branch.

Let
\begin{equation}
s_f^+(x)
=
\operatorname{sgn}\!\left((x-q^+)^\top n_f^+\right)
\end{equation}
denote the geometric sign induced by the warped mesh normal. When source
TSDF samples are available on both sides of the paired source face, their
central difference determines whether this geometric sign is retained or
flipped. Otherwise, the sign of a consistent source sample is used when
available, with $s_f^+(x)$ as the final fallback. Denote the resulting sign
by $s_{\rm tr}(x)$.

Source-supported samples satisfying $r(x)\leq\rho$ define the near-surface
support. Source support farther from the surface, up to $\tau$, is retained
only when it forms a sign-consistent, face-connected continuation of this
support. The accepted samples form $\Omega_{\rm tr}$, with
\begin{equation}
\bigl(
\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),
w_{\rm tr}^+(x)
\bigr)
=
\bigl(
s_{\rm tr}(x)d^+(x),
w_{\rm src}^+(x)
\bigr).
\label{eq:transferred_tsdf}
\end{equation}

Where transferred source support is unavailable, voxels satisfying
$r(x)\leq\rho$ receive a finite surface prior, unless their closest surface
feature is an open boundary:
\begin{equation}
w_{\rm fb}(x)
=
\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}
\left(
\frac{\rho-r(x)}{s}
\right).
\label{eq:fallback_weight}
\end{equation}
This prior provides compact support for continued fusion and is not
interpreted as recovered observation count. Its supported voxels form
$\Omega_{\rm fb}$.
The surface-local reconstruction does not recover observed free space
farther from the corrected zero set. Observed source voxels satisfying
$w^-(y)>0$ and $\Phi^-(y)\geq\rho$ are eligible for forward transport. They
are partitioned into truncation-scale groups, and only groups admitting a
valid paired-surface frame are transported. For group $c$, let $q_c^\pm$
and $R_c^\pm$ denote its corresponding source and warped points and frames.
The forward map is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_c(y)
=
q_c^+
+
R_c^+(R_c^-)^\top(y-q_c^-).
\label{eq:forward_free_space}
\end{equation}
Transported samples may allocate additional output blocks. They retain
their positive source-derived values and weights, fill only voxels that
remain unsupported after surface-local reconstruction, and are rejected
when they would introduce a new zero crossing adjacent to retained
negative support. The accepted samples form $\Omega_{\rm fs}$, with values
and weights $(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+,w_{\rm fs}^+)$. Fallback is assigned only where transferred source support is unavailable,
and the subsequent free-space pass writes only to voxels that remain
unsupported. Hence, $\Omega_{\rm tr}$, $\Omega_{\rm fb}$, and
$\Omega_{\rm fs}$ are disjoint. With
$\Omega^+=\Omega_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}\cup\Omega_{\rm fs}$, the
corrected field is
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^+(x),w^+(x))
=
\begin{cases}
(\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),w_{\rm tr}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
(s_f^+(x)d^+(x),w_{\rm fb}(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fb},\\
(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+(x),w_{\rm fs}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fs}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_tsdf}
\end{equation}
Voxels outside $\Omega^+$ have zero weight. When the source TSDF is
unavailable, reconstruction reduces to the finite surface prior.
The resulting native Voxblox volume replaces the active map, allowing
subsequent fusion and later corrections to operate directly on the updated
TSDF.


\subsection{Complexity and Scope}

Let $Q$ be the number of surface -- keyframe candidate projections,
$A=|\mathcal A|$ the number of active source cells, $P=|P^-|$ the proxy
size, and $X^-$ and $X^+$ the source and output voxel slots processed in
Stage~4. The per-correction cost is
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm corr}
=
O(K_{\rm KF}+Q)
+
C_{\rm def}(A,P)
+
C_{\rm vol}(X^-,X^+).
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
For $V_q=O(A)$ queried vertices and
$\bar k=Q/V_q$ candidate keyframes per vertex,
$Q=O(A\bar k)$, with worst case $O(AK_{\rm KF})$; the standard proxy is
bounded by $P\leq P_{\max}\approx60\,\mathrm K$.

Full reintegration costs
$C_{\rm reint}=\Theta(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|D_n|)=\Theta(N|D|)$.
When $K_{\rm KF}=\Theta(N)$, the frame-count order is unchanged, but
WarpVox avoids volumetric integration of the $|D|$ range samples in each
historical frame.
