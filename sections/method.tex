\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
Following the local-consistency assumption of
Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, we assume that TSDF fusion under the
drifted pre-correction trajectory remains locally valid: surface geometry and
connectivity are coherent in local neighborhoods although the map is globally
inconsistent. WarpVox corrects this global inconsistency after loop closure;
severely noisy or topologically corrupted source maps are outside its scope.

\subsection{Problem and Correction Inputs} Let $T^-(t),T^+(t)\in SE(3)$ map the sensor frame at time $t$ to the world frame before and after trajectory correction. The map built with $T^-$ consists of a TSDF $\Phi^-$, its weight field $w^-$, voxel size $s$, and truncation distance $\tau$. During online fusion, Voxblox continuously maintains the Marching-Cubes surface $\mathcal M^-=(V^-,F)$ of the active TSDF~\cite{10.1145/37402.37422}. On a trajectory correction, this mesh becomes the source surface. Let $\mathcal A$ denote the active TSDF cells that contribute triangles to $\mathcal M^-$. WarpVox stores the timestamped, modality-native range images of SLAM keyframes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\rm KF}=\{k_i=(t_i,D_i):i\in{\rm KF}\},
\end{equation}
where ${\rm KF}$ indexes the retained SLAM keyframes and $D_i$ is an RGB-D depth image or a spherical LiDAR range image. Together with $T^-$, these measurements form the observation input to Stage~1. The measurements remain available across correction events. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the four stages.

Its contract is
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^-,w^-,\mathcal M^-,T^+,\mathcal O)
\longmapsto (\Phi^+,w^+),
\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
where the warped mesh is intermediate and $(\Phi^+,w^+)$ is a
native Voxblox TSDF volume.

\subsection{Stage 1: Correspondence Establishment}

Stage~1 establishes projective correspondences between the current source
surface and the keyframe range measurements under the pre-correction
trajectory. For each queried source vertex $v_j^-$ with normal $n_j^-$, a
spatial index over sensor positions retrieves candidate keyframes that may
observe it.
Viewing incidence is evaluated in the pre-correction world frame from the
source normal $n_j^-$ and the sensor-to-vertex direction.

Let $r$ index a candidate measurement with timestamp $t_r$ and range image
$D_r$. The source vertex is transformed into the corresponding sensor frame
and sampled in the measurement domain:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat v_{rj}^-
&=
\left[
[T^-(t_r)]^{-1}\widetilde v_j^-
\right]_{1:3},\\
m_{rj}
&=
\mathcal S_m\!\left(
D_r,\Pi_m(\hat v_{rj}^-)
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sensor_sample}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi_m$ is the pinhole projection for RGB-D or the spherical projection
for LiDAR. For RGB-D, $\mathcal S_m$ samples the projected depth and
back-projects it to the sensor frame. For LiDAR, it selects the closest valid
return in a local neighbourhood of the projected spherical-image location.

The sampled point is checked against the projected source vertex. Euclidean
distance measures their complete three-dimensional agreement, while
depth/range consistency compares $z$-depth for RGB-D and radial range for
LiDAR. Viewing incidence is evaluated in the pre-correction world frame
from the source normal $n_j^-$ and the sensor-to-vertex direction.

A valid association receives reliability $\alpha_{rj}$ based on geometric
proximity, viewing incidence, and range attenuation; RGB-D additionally
accounts for projective depth consistency. This reliability ranks multiple
observations of the same source vertex and enters the target aggregation in
Stage~2. The verified tuples $(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})$ form
$\mathcal O_j$, and $\mathcal O=\{\mathcal O_j\}_j$ is passed to Stage~2.


\subsection{Stage 2: Corrected Surface Targets}

The sensor-frame sample $m_{rj}$ is unchanged by a later revision of the
world-frame trajectory. Let $\widehat T_r^+$ denote the corrected pose
associated with record $r$. RGB-D records are resolved by timestamp, while LiDAR records use
the corresponding scan pose. The corrected candidate is
\begin{equation}
q_{rj}^+
=
\left[
\widehat T_r^+\widetilde m_{rj}
\right]_{1:3}.
\label{eq:corrected_target}
\end{equation}

Let
$\mathcal J_j=\{r:(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})\in\mathcal O_j\}$.
The Stage~1 reliability gives the initial target estimate
\begin{equation}
\bar q_j
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target_initial}
\end{equation}

A median-relative residual threshold selects a consistent subset:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_j
&=
\max\!\left(
\epsilon_m,\,
\kappa\operatorname*{median}_{r\in\mathcal J_j}
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|
\right),\\
I_j
&=
\left\{
r\in\mathcal J_j:
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|\le\theta_j
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:target_inliers}
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon_m$ reflects the modality's sampling resolution and $\kappa$
is shared across modalities. When fewer than three candidates are available,
no trimming is applied. The final observation-derived target is
\begin{equation}
v_j^*
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

Each supported source vertex yields one target displacement
$v_j^*-v_j^-$ for Stage~3.

\subsection{Stage 3: Surface Deformation}

The deformation stage propagates observation-derived motion; it does not
infer correction from shape alone. Let $d_j=v_j^*-v_j^-$ be a control
displacement. A component-aware inverse-distance predictor estimates each
control displacement from valid neighbours, and a fixed median -- MAD test
rejects isolated residuals. Small fragments retained only by controls that
are subsequently rejected are discarded. Other components without a
surviving control receive synthetic constraints carrying the coordinate-wise
median displacement of nearby accepted controls; these constraints define
otherwise unconstrained motion but are not treated as observations.

Component-separated spatial clustering~\cite{rossignac1993vertexclustering}
constructs a triangular proxy $\mathcal P^-=(P^-,F_P)$ of approximately
60K vertices, selecting one QEM representative~\cite{garland1997qem} per
occupied cell. Let $\pi:V^-\rightarrow P^-$ map source vertices to proxy
representatives. Controls mapped to proxy vertex $a$ are averaged as
$p_a^{(0)}=p_a^-+|\mathcal K_a|^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal K_a}d_j$, where
$\mathcal K_a=\{j:\pi(j)=a\}$.

A Welsch-weighted rigid transform
~\cite{schoenemann1966procrustes,yao2020welsch} is fitted to neighbouring
proxy source -- target pairs. An observation-derived target is replaced by its
fitted prediction only when its residual exceeds a fixed robust cutoff and a strict majority of the
evaluated observation-derived targets in the same connected component exceed
the same cutoff; isolated disagreements remain unchanged. Synthetic
targets are softly regularized by the same local field. Let $\mathcal C_P$
index the resulting measured or synthetic targets $p_a^*$.

WarpVox performs one proxy ARAP solve per correction~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007, 10.1007/978-3-662-05105-4_2,libigl}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{P^+,\{R_a\in SO(3)\}}\quad
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{|P^-|}
\sum_{b\in\mathcal N_P(a)}c_{ab}\\[-1mm]
&\left\|(p_a^+-p_b^+)-R_a(p_a^--p_b^-)\right\|^2,\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&p_a^+=p_a^*,\qquad a\in\mathcal C_P .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:arap}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal N_P(a)$ is the proxy one-ring and $c_{ab}$ are symmetric
cotangent-derived weights. The accepted targets are imposed as exact
constraints, and standard local -- global iterations solve one deformation per
correction.

Proxy displacement is transferred to $V^-$ by topology-local barycentric
interpolation. Let $f_j$ be the closest triangle to $v_j^-$ in
$\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$. With barycentric coordinates $\lambda_{jb}$ of
its closest point,
\begin{equation}
v_j^+=v_j^-+\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}(p_b^+-p_b^-),
\qquad \lambda_{jb}\ge0,\quad\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}=1.
\label{eq:proxy_transfer}
\end{equation}
Restricting $f_j$ to $\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$ prevents displacement
transfer across nearby disconnected sheets. Faces with excessive edge
stretch, area expansion, or aspect-ratio distortion define a repair region.
Their displacements are harmonically interpolated from surrounding unflagged
vertices, and faces with unresolved edge or area expansion are removed. The
pass creates no new connections and performs no retriangulation. Consequently,
the retained source and warped meshes share the same indexed face subset:
$\bar{\mathcal M}^-=(\bar V^-,\bar F)$ and
$\mathcal M^+=(\bar V^+,\bar F)$, with $\bar F\subseteq F$.

\subsection{Stage 4: Source-Supported TSDF Reconstruction}

The warped surface supplies the corrected zero set
~\cite{sussman1999redistancing}, while the source TSDF supplies observed
support, sign orientation, integration weights, and free space. Let
$\mathcal B_V^+$ be the blocks intersected by $\bar V^+$,
$\mathcal B_{\rm fs}^+$ the additional blocks reached by transported free
space, and $\operatorname{vox}(b)$ the voxel centres in block $b$. WarpVox
allocates only
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B^+=\mathcal N_1(\mathcal B_V^+)\cup\mathcal B_{\rm fs}^+,
\qquad
\mathcal X^+=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal B^+}\operatorname{vox}(b),
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_1$ is a one-block dilation. Thus, no scene-wide bounding
box is voxelized.

For $x\in\mathcal X^+$, a closest-triangle
query~\cite{10.1145/2601097.2601199} on $\mathcal M^+$ returns face $f$,
closest point $q^+$, and barycentric coordinates $\beta$. Shared face
indices give the corresponding source point
$q^-=\sum_{k\in f}\beta_k\bar v_k^-$. Let $R_f^\pm\in SO(3)$ be consistently
oriented frames built from the same indexed triangle edge and face normal in
the source and warped meshes. The piecewise local-rigid pullback
\begin{equation}
\Psi_f(x)=q^-+R_f^-(R_f^+)^\top(x-q^+)
\label{eq:inverse_map}
\end{equation}
preserves tangential and normal offsets between the paired
triangles~\cite{sumner2004deformationtransfer}.

\noindent\textbf{Local field construction.}
Let $x^-=\Psi_f(x)$, $r(x)=\|x-q^+\|$, and
$d^+(x)=\min(\tau,r(x))$. The trilinear source sample
$(\widetilde\Phi^-,\widetilde w^-)(x^-)$ is valid when every source voxel
with a nonzero interpolation coefficient has positive weight. We define
$w_{\rm src}^+(x)$ as its interpolated weight; if the complete sample is
unavailable, the positive weight of the source voxel containing $x^-$ is
used without copying its distance value.

Let $s_f^+(x)=\operatorname{sgn}((x-q^+)^\top n_f^+)$ be the oriented
warped-face sign. When valid source samples exist on both sides of the paired
source face, their central difference orients $n_f^+$; otherwise, within the
surface-local band, a sign-consistent trilinear sample selects the sign, with
$s_f^+(x)$ as the final fallback. Denote the resulting sign by
$s_{\rm tr}(x)$.

Source-supported samples within $1.5s$ provide surface-local support; farther
samples up to $\tau$ are retained only when sign-consistent with their active
6-neighbours and connected to the surface-local band under 6-connectivity.
Their accepted set is $\Omega_{\rm tr}$, where
$(\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),w_{\rm tr}^+(x))=
(s_{\rm tr}(x)d^+(x),w_{\rm src}^+(x))$.
Where source support is unavailable, voxels within $1.5s$ whose closest
surface feature is not an open boundary form $\Omega_{\rm fb}$ and receive
$w_{\rm fb}(x)=\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(1.5-r(x)/s)$.
The fallback provides only finite support for continued fusion and is not
treated as recovered observation count.

To preserve observed free space beyond the surface-local band, source voxels
satisfying $w^-(y)>0$ and $\Phi^-(y)\ge\eta_{\rm fs}$ are partitioned into
truncation-scale local groups and transported forward using the paired source
and warped surface frames. For group $c$, let $q_c^-$ and $q_c^+$ be
corresponding points on the paired triangles and let $R_c^-$ and $R_c^+$ be
their local frames. The forward map is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_c(y)=q_c^+ + R_c^+(R_c^-)^\top(y-q_c^-).
\label{eq:forward_free_space}
\end{equation}
Mapped samples may allocate additional output blocks. They are written only
into previously unsupported voxels and are rejected when they would introduce
a new zero crossing adjacent to retained occupied support. The accepted
samples define $\Omega_{\rm fs}$ and retain positive, source-derived TSDF
values and weights $(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+,w_{\rm fs}^+)$.

Since fallback and free-space transport write only to unsupported voxels,
the three support branches are disjoint. With
$\Omega^+=\Omega_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}\cup\Omega_{\rm fs}$,
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^+(x),w^+(x))=
\begin{cases}
(\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),w_{\rm tr}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
(s_f^+(x)d^+(x),w_{\rm fb}(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fb},\\
(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+(x),w_{\rm fs}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fs}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_tsdf}
\end{equation}
Voxels outside $\Omega^+$ have zero weight.

Without a source TSDF, WarpVox uses only the finite-band prior around the
Stage-3 surface. The result is serialized as a native Voxblox TSDF and
replaces the active volume, after which standard fusion and later corrections
continue from the updated state. Its MC mesh is used only for evaluation and
visualization.

\subsection{Complexity and Scope}
Let $A=|\mathcal A|$, $K_{\rm KF}=|\mathcal C_{\rm KF}|$, $P=|P^-|$, and
$X^+=|\mathcal X^+|$ be the numbers of active source cells, stored keyframe
range images, proxy vertices, and allocated output voxels. At fixed
resolution, MC yields a constant number of surface elements per active cell,
so $|V^-|=O(A)$; storage is $O(\sum_{i\in{\rm KF}}|D_i|)$ for the
measurements and $O(A)$ for the per-vertex association state. With
$\kappa\le K_{\rm KF}$ the maximum number of keyframes returned for one source
vertex, association costs
$O(K_{\rm KF}\log K_{\rm KF}+A(\log K_{\rm KF}+\kappa))$ for RGB-D and
$O(K_{\rm KF}+A(1+\kappa))$ for LiDAR under the standard output-sensitive
index model.

The total correction cost is
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm corr}=C_{\rm assoc}+C_{\rm ARAP}(P,F_P)+C_{\rm vol}(X^+,\bar F),
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\rm vol}$ is the Stage-4 volumetric reconstruction cost. The
block-local allocation in \eqref{eq:allocation} makes $X^+$ follow the
warped-surface footprint and transported source support rather than scene
extent, and the proxy contains approximately 60K vertices. The retained
association state per surface element is bounded by a constant, and each
output voxel is written once rather than once per contributing measurement.

Full reintegration instead re-fuses every historical measurement at
$O(N|D|)$. The two costs are indexed by different quantities: the keyframes
and active map for WarpVox, and the measurement history for reintegration.
