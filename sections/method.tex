\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

WarpVox corrects an existing truncated signed distance field (TSDF) after
loop closure, using stored keyframe measurements and the externally
corrected trajectory. TSDF fusion forms each surface point from the range
measurements that observed it, placed by the poses of their frames; a loop
closure revises those poses but leaves the measurements valid in their
sensor frames. WarpVox traces the fusion backwards: Stage~1 recovers, for
each surface vertex, the keyframe measurements that formed it; Stage~2
places them under the corrected poses to obtain observation-derived
targets; Stage~3 propagates these targets over the surface; and Stage~4
combines the corrected geometry with source-derived support, sign, weights,
and free space, returning a native TSDF for continued fusion.

\subsection{Problem and Correction Inputs}
\label{sec:problem}

Following Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, the pre-correction TSDF is
assumed locally valid: surface geometry and connectivity stay coherent in
local neighbourhoods despite global inconsistency, and severely noisy or
topologically corrupted source maps are outside our scope.

For $x\in\mathbb R^3$, $\widetilde x=[x^\top,1]^\top$ denotes homogeneous
coordinates. $T^-(t),T^+(t)\in SE(3)$ map the sensor frame at time $t$ to the
world frame before and after the correction.

The map built with $T^-$ is a Voxblox TSDF~\cite{Oleynikova_2017}: a regular
grid of voxel size $s$, allocated sparsely in blocks. Each
voxel centre $x$ stores a truncated signed distance
$\Phi^-(x)\in[-\tau,\tau]$, positive on the sensor side, with truncation
distance $\tau$, and an integration weight $w^-(x)\ge0$. Unallocated voxels
have zero weight. A voxel is \emph{supported} if its weight is positive; the
supported voxels form the observed support $\Omega^-$.

At a correction, the Marching-Cubes surface~\cite{10.1145/37402.37422} that
Voxblox maintains during fusion becomes the source surface
$\mathcal M^-=(V^-,F)$, an indexed triangle mesh: $V^-=\{v_j^-\}$ are the
vertex positions, $F$ the triangle indices, and $n_j^-$ the area-weighted
vertex normal.

WarpVox stores, per keyframe (KF), one timestamped range image in its sensor
frame, $\mathcal C_{\rm KF}=\{(t_i,D_i)\}$: the depth image of each SLAM
keyframe (RGB-D), or the spherical range image of each posed scan (LiDAR).
Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the four stages, and
Table~\ref{tab:params} lists all parameters. Figs.~\ref{fig:mech_assoc}--\ref{fig:mech_recon}
illustrate association, clustering, and reconstructed support.

\subsection{Stage 1: Correspondence Establishment}
\label{sec:stage1}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{Figures/mech_assoc.pdf}
  \caption{Stage~1: observation association.}
  \label{fig:mech_assoc}
\end{figure}

Stage~1 recovers the measurements that formed the surface: it associates source vertices with
geometrically consistent keyframe measurements under $T^-$, the poses used
to build the map. For each queried
vertex $v_j^-$, a $k$-d tree (RGB-D) or grid (LiDAR) over the sensor centres
retrieves candidate keyframes within the index radius.

For candidate $r$, with timestamp $t_r$ and range image $D_r$, the vertex is
mapped into its sensor frame with the old pose and sampled:
\begin{equation}
\hat v_{rj}^-=\bigl[[T^-(t_r)]^{-1}\widetilde v_j^-\bigr]_{1:3},\qquad
m_{rj}=\mathcal S_m\bigl(D_r,\Pi_m(\hat v_{rj}^-)\bigr).
\label{eq:sensor_sample}
\end{equation}
Here, for modality $m$, $\Pi_m$ is the pinhole (RGB-D) or spherical (LiDAR)
projection, and $\mathcal S_m$ returns one point $m_{rj}\in\mathbb R^3$ in the
sensor frame, or none: the depth bilinearly interpolated from at least two
valid pixels, back-projected along the same pixel ray (RGB-D), or the stored
return closest to $\hat v_{rj}^-$ in a $3{\times}3$ pixel window (LiDAR).

Let $z_{rj}^-$ and $z_{rj}^m$ be the depth (RGB-D) or range (LiDAR) of
$\hat v_{rj}^-$ and $m_{rj}$, $\delta_{rj}=\|m_{rj}-\hat v_{rj}^-\|$ their
distance, and $\phi_{rj}$ the angle between $n_j^-$ and the viewing ray to
$v_j^-$ under $T^-$. The measurement must agree with the vertex within the
depth-dependent gate $\delta_{rj}\le g_0+g_1z_{rj}^-$, and, for LiDAR,
$|z_{rj}^m-z_{rj}^-|$ must meet the same bound. A passing sample receives the
reliability
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{rj}=\frac{|\cos\phi_{rj}|}{1+z_{rj}^-/z_0}\,
e^{-\delta_{rj}/\sigma_g}\,e^{-|z_{rj}^m-z_{rj}^-|/\sigma_z},
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
with range scale $z_0$ and discrepancy scales $\sigma_g,\sigma_z$; the last
factor applies to RGB-D only.

A candidate is retained if $\mathcal S_m$ returns a sample,
the gate holds, and, for RGB-D, $\alpha_{rj}\ge\alpha_{\min}$.
Each vertex keeps at most $n_{\max}$ retained candidates, those of largest
$\alpha_{rj}$, as its \emph{observations}
$(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})\in\mathcal O_j$. A vertex with
$\mathcal O_j\neq\emptyset$ is \emph{observed}; unobserved vertices follow
the Stage-3 deformation without a measured target.

\subsection{Stage 2: Corrected Surface Targets}
\label{sec:stage2}

The matched measurements retain their sensor-frame coordinates when the
trajectory changes. Stage~2 transforms them with the corrected poses and
aggregates them into one target per observed vertex. In both modalities,
$\widehat T_r^+$ is the corrected keyframe pose, retrieved by timestamp
$t_r$ (RGB-D, interpolated if needed) or scan index (LiDAR). The
\emph{corrected sample} is
\begin{equation}
q_{rj}^+=\bigl[\widehat T_r^+\widetilde m_{rj}\bigr]_{1:3}.
\label{eq:corrected_target}
\end{equation}

Let $\mathcal J_j$ index the observations of $v_j^-$ for which this pose
exists. Each is weighted by its reliability, damped with scale $\zeta_m$ by
depth (RGB-D), $\omega_{rj}=\alpha_{rj}/(1+z_{rj}^m/\zeta_m)$, or by
displacement (LiDAR), $\omega_{rj}=\alpha_{rj}/(1+\|q_{rj}^+-v_j^-\|/\zeta_m)$.
With $\bar q_j$ the $\omega$-weighted mean of $q_{rj}^+$ over
$\mathcal J_j$, samples farther than
$\theta_j=\max(\epsilon_m,\kappa\operatorname{median}_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|)$
from it are dropped, leaving $I_j$; $\epsilon_m$ is a floor at the sampling
resolution of the modality, $\kappa>1$ keeps $I_j$ nonempty for nonempty
$\mathcal J_j$, and with fewer than three samples none is dropped. The
retained samples give the observation-derived target
\begin{equation}
v_j^*=\frac{\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}q_{rj}^+}{\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}},
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
A vertex with $\mathcal J_j=\emptyset$ is treated as unobserved. Each observed vertex supplies
the displacement $d_j=v_j^*-v_j^-$ to Stage~3.


\subsection{Stage 3: Surface Deformation}
\label{sec:stage3}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{Figures/mech_cluster.pdf}
  \caption{Stage~3: component-aware clustering.}
  \label{fig:mech_cluster}
\end{figure}

Stage~3 uses a compact, component-aware proxy for efficient deformation of
large meshes and transfers its motion to the source surface. The resulting
source and warped meshes retain shared triangles. The as-rigid-as-possible
(ARAP) energy~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007} penalizes local shape distortion
while enforcing prescribed positions at \emph{controls}; observed vertices
provide \emph{measured controls}. Mesh edges define the connected components,
which are relabelled at each correction; \emph{fragments} are components with
fewer than $N_{\rm f}$ faces. Algorithm~\ref{alg:stage3} orders steps (a)--(f).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Stage~3 of one correction}
\label{alg:stage3}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require source mesh $(V^-,F)$; measured controls $d_j$
\Ensure paired surfaces $\bar{\mathcal M}^-,\mathcal M^+$
\State label components; remove degenerate faces and control-free fragments
\State (a) remove outlier controls once
\State (b) omit fragments without controls; add synthetic controls
\If{$|V^-|>1.3P_{\max}$}
  \State (c) build proxy $\mathcal P^-$; (d) proxy targets, Eq.~\eqref{eq:welsch}
  \State (e) solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:arap} on $\mathcal P^-$; (f) transfer to source
\Else
  \State (e) solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:arap} on the source mesh
\EndIf
\For{at most $n_{\rm rep}$ passes} \Comment{(f)}
  \State relax invalid faces; stop if none or no progress
\EndFor
\State remove faces violating $\tau_{\rm e}$ or $\tau_{\rm A}$ and omitted components
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textit{(a) Outlier controls.}
Neighbourhoods favour same-component controls to respect separate surface motions.
Among the $k$ nearest other controls, $\mathcal N_j$ retains same-component
neighbours if at least $n_{\min}$ exist, and all $k$ otherwise. The
prediction $\hat d_j$ is their mean displacement with weights
$\|v_j^--v_i^-\|^{-2}$, and $e_j=\|d_j-\hat d_j\|$ is the residual. With $\mu$
the median of the residuals and $\mathrm{MAD}=\operatorname{median}_j|e_j-\mu|$,
control $j$ is an \emph{outlier} and is removed if
$e_j>\mu+\gamma\max(1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD},\sigma_{\min})$, where $\gamma$ is the
rejection multiplier, $\sigma_{\min}$ the scale floor, and $1.4826\,$MAD the
robust scale estimate~\cite{rousseeuw1993mad}.

\textit{(b) Components without controls.}
A fragment whose controls were all removed is \emph{omitted} from the output.
Every other component without a surviving control receives three
\emph{synthetic controls} at mutually distant vertices to anchor its rigid
motion. They take the coordinate-wise median
displacement of the $k$ surviving controls nearest to its centroid.

\textit{(c) Proxy.}
A mesh with more than $1.3P_{\max}$ vertices is deformed through a coarse
proxy $\mathcal P^-=(P^-,F_P)$ built by vertex
clustering~\cite{rossignac1993vertexclustering}. Vertices are binned into
cubic cells of side $h=(A_{\mathcal M}/P_{\max})^{1/2}$, with
$A_{\mathcal M}$ the mesh area, which yields about $P_{\max}$ proxy vertices.
A cluster is a (cell, component) pair and lies within one component.
Each cluster becomes one proxy vertex $p_a^-$, placed with the quadric error
metric (QEM)~\cite{garland1997qem}; $\pi(j)$ is the proxy vertex of $v_j^-$.
This preserves local surface geometry and keeps disconnected surfaces
separate in the reduced solve.

\textit{(d) Proxy targets.}
The initial target of proxy vertex $a$ is its rest position plus the mean
displacement of the controls clustered into it,
$p_a^{(0)}=p_a^-+|\mathcal K_a|^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal K_a}d_j$ with
$\mathcal K_a=\{j\in\mathcal C:\pi(j)=a\}$, where $\mathcal C$ holds the
surviving measured and synthetic controls.
Vertex $a$ is \emph{controlled} if $\mathcal K_a\neq\emptyset$; a proxy
component without such a vertex has three vertices controlled at
$p_a^{(0)}=p_a^-$. $\mathcal M_P$ is the set of controlled vertices that
hold a measured control; the targets of the others are \emph{synthetic}.
Local rigid fits consolidate the proxy targets before deformation.
For each controlled $a$, a rigid motion $(\hat R_a,\hat t_a)$ is fitted to
the pairs $(p_b^-,p_b^{(0)})$ of its $k$ nearest vertices
$b\in\mathcal M_P\setminus\{a\}$, from any component, by Procrustes
alignment~\cite{schoenemann1966procrustes}. The fit is iteratively
reweighted by the Welsch weights
$\nu(r_b)=e^{-r_b^2/2\sigma_a^2}$~\cite{yao2020welsch} of the residuals
$r_b=\|p_b^{(0)}-\hat R_ap_b^--\hat t_a\|$, times a fixed prior favouring
near, distance-preserving, and well-observed neighbours; $\sigma_a$ is the
median of the $r_b$ plus 1.4826 times their MAD. With the prediction
$\hat p_a=\hat R_ap_a^-+\hat t_a$ and $r_a=\|p_a^{(0)}-\hat p_a\|$, a
measured target is \emph{gross} if $r_a\ge c_{\rm g}\sigma_a$. Of the $N_K$
fitted measured targets in component $K$, $G_K$ are gross; $2G_K>N_K$
denotes a strict majority. The constrained target is
\begin{equation}
p_a^*=\begin{cases}
\hat p_a, & a\ \text{gross},\ 2G_K>N_K,\\
\hat p_a+\nu(r_a)(p_a^{(0)}-\hat p_a), & a\notin\mathcal M_P,\\
p_a^{(0)}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:welsch}
\end{equation}
A vertex with too few neighbours for a fit keeps $p_a^{(0)}$.

\textit{(e) ARAP.}
Let $P^+=\{p_a^+\}$ be the deformed proxy positions; controlled vertices,
indexed by $\mathcal C_P$, are fixed to $p_a^*$, and the others are the
unknowns. With one rotation $R_a\in SO(3)$ per proxy vertex, we solve
\begin{equation}
\min_{P^+,\{R_a\}}\sum_{a}\!\sum_{f\in\operatorname{star}_P(a)}
\sum_{(b,c)\in f}\!\tfrac{c^f_{bc}}{3}
\|(p_b^+-p_c^+)-R_a(p_b^--p_c^-)\|^2,
\label{eq:arap}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{star}_P(a)$ is the one-ring of proxy triangles incident to
$a$, and $(b,c)$ runs over their edges, so $R_a$ fits both spokes and
rims~\cite{chao2010elastic}. The
weight $c^f_{bc}=\tfrac12\cot\vartheta^f_{bc}$ uses the angle
$\vartheta^f_{bc}$ opposite edge $(b,c)$ in rest triangle $f$. Summing over
incident faces gives the symmetric cotangent weight
$c_{bc}=\sum_{f\supset(b,c)}c^f_{bc}$~\cite{10.1007/978-3-662-05105-4_2}.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:arap} is solved with
libigl~\cite{libigl} by a fixed number of local--global iterations.

\textit{(f) Transfer and repair.}
Each source vertex takes the proxy motion \emph{topology-locally}: $f_j$ is
the rest triangle of $\operatorname{star}_P(\pi(j))$ closest to $v_j^-$, and
$\lambda_{jb}$ are the barycentric coordinates of the closest point, so
$v_j^+=v_j^-+\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}(p_b^+-p_b^-)$, using triangles
within the vertex's own component. Local repair regularizes distorted
triangles. For a face
with source and warped edge lengths $\ell_e^\pm$, longest warped edge
$\ell^+_{\max}$, and areas $A^\pm$, the edge stretch
$\xi^{\rm e}=\max_e\ell^+_e/\ell^-_e$, area growth $\xi^{\rm A}=A^+/A^-$, and
aspect ratio $\xi^{\rm asp}=(\ell^+_{\max})^2/(2A^+)$ measure its distortion. A face is
\emph{invalid} if $\xi^{\rm e}\ge\tau_{\rm e}$, $\xi^{\rm A}\ge\tau_{\rm A}$, or
$\xi^{\rm asp}\ge\tau_{\rm asp}$ with $\ell^+_{\max}\ge\ell_{\min}$.
Jacobi relaxation smooths the displacements of their vertices with all
other vertices fixed. The triangles retained by Algorithm~\ref{alg:stage3},
$\bar F\subseteq F$, define the paired surfaces $\bar{\mathcal M}^-=(\bar V^-,\bar F)$ and
$\mathcal M^+=(\bar V^+,\bar F)$, which the shared face indices pair.


\subsection{Stage 4: Source-Supported TSDF Reconstruction}
\label{sec:stage4}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{Figures/mech_recon.pdf}
  \caption{Stage~4: transferred, fallback, and free-space support.}
  \label{fig:mech_recon}
\end{figure}

Stage~4 restores the volumetric state needed for continued fusion, using
the source grid resolution. The warped surface $\mathcal M^+$ provides the
zero set and distance magnitudes; the source TSDF $(\Phi^-,w^-)$ supplies
observed support and accumulated integration weights and guides the sign.

\emph{Surface-local reconstruction} handles the voxel centres $x$ within
$\tau$ of $\mathcal M^+$ in the blocks that hold a warped vertex and their 26
neighbours. For each $x$, a closest-triangle
query~\cite{10.1145/2601097.2601199} on $\mathcal M^+$ returns face
$f=(a,b,c)$, closest point $q^+$, and barycentric coordinates $\beta$; the
same coordinates on the source triangle give
$q^-=\sum_{k\in f}\beta_k\bar v_k^-$. Let $n_f^\pm$ be the unit normal of $f$
on $\bar{\mathcal M}^-$ and $\mathcal M^+$, oriented by the vertex order of
$F$, and $t_f^\pm$ the unit direction of edge $(a,b)$. The frames
$R_f^\pm\in SO(3)$ with columns $t_f^\pm$, $n_f^\pm\times t_f^\pm$, $n_f^\pm$
define the local-rigid pullback
\begin{equation}
\Psi_f(x)=q^-+R_f^-(R_f^+)^\top(x-q^+).
\label{eq:inverse_map}
\end{equation}
This maps the surface-relative offset into the source triangle's frame for
source-state queries~\cite{sumner2004deformationtransfer}; let $x^-=\Psi_f(x)$,
$r(x)=\|x-q^+\|$, and $d^+(x)=\min(\tau,r(x))$.

A trilinear source sample at $x^-$ is \emph{valid} if every source voxel with
a nonzero coefficient is supported, and \emph{sign-consistent} if their
distances also share one nonzero sign. The transferred weight is
\begin{equation}
w_{\rm src}^+(x)=\begin{cases}
\bar w^-(x^-), & \text{valid sample at } x^-,\\
w^-(y_0(x^-)), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:source_weight}
\end{equation}
capped at $w_{\max}$, with $\bar w^-$ the interpolated weight and $y_0(x^-)$
the source voxel containing $x^-$. Voxel $x$ is
\emph{source-supported} if $w_{\rm src}^+(x)>0$. The warped normal gives the
geometric sign $s_f^+(x)=\operatorname{sgn}((x-q^+)^\top n_f^+)$, and the
source TSDF decides the transferred sign
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm tr}(x)=\begin{cases}
s_f^+(x)\operatorname{sgn}(g_f), & g_f\ \text{available},\\
\operatorname{sgn}\bar\Phi^-(x^-), & \text{else, if sign-consistent},\\
s_f^+(x), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:sign}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\Phi^-$ is the interpolated source distance and
$g_f=[\bar\Phi^-(q^-+s\,n_f^-)-\bar\Phi^-(q^--s\,n_f^-)]/(2s)$ its central
difference across the paired source face. It calibrates the normal toward
free space and is available when all three source samples are valid and
$g_f\neq0$.

Within the surface band, \emph{transferred} voxels are source-supported.
Those within the near-surface
margin, $r(x)\le\rho$, form the narrow transferred support $\mathcal N_{\rm tr}$; those with
$\rho<r(x)\le\tau$ form the extended set $\mathcal E$, which keeps a voxel
with a valid sample only if $g_f$ is available.

\emph{Fallback} supplies a surface prior where the pullback query has no
source support:
$w_{\rm src}^+(x)=0$ and $r(x)\le\rho$. These voxels take the prior weight
$w_{\rm fb}(x)=\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}((\rho-r(x))/s)$.
The prior is withheld where $q^+$ lies on a boundary edge (incident to one
face) or its endpoint. The voxels with $w_{\rm fb}(x)>0$ form $\Omega_{\rm fb}$.

Reconstruction filters sign conflicts and disconnected extended support.
A voxel of $\mathcal E$ is
removed if a supported face neighbour has the opposite sign, which Marching
Cubes would read as a surface. A flood fill through face-connected voxels of
$\mathcal E$, started from $\mathcal N_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}$, then
removes the voxels of $\mathcal E$ it does not reach; $\mathcal N_{\rm tr}$
and the remaining voxels of $\mathcal E$ form $\Omega_{\rm tr}$.

\emph{Observed-volume transport} carries the source TSDF state through the
same paired-frame construction. All observed source voxels $y\in\Omega^-$
are grouped into truncation-sized cells. Each cell $c$ uses the source face
closest to its observed-voxel centroid, unless it or its warped copy is
degenerate, and the paired warped face to define
$\Gamma_c(y)=q_c^++R_c^+(R_c^-)^\top(y-q_c^-)$.
Output voxel centres are sampled at $\Gamma_c^{-1}(x)$ using trilinear
interpolation where valid, and the containing source voxel otherwise.
At each output voxel, repeated samples from one source-connected region
contribute once; distinct regions fuse by a weighted mean of distances and
a sum of weights.
On the reconstructed band, the distance is retained and contributions
already represented by its pullback are excluded from this sum. The
remaining contributions supply an additional weight $w_{\rm add}^+(x)$.

\emph{Surface preservation} constrains transported distances to fill empty
output voxels. Candidates with an opposite-sign reconstructed voxel among
their 26 neighbours are excluded. Opposite-sign candidate neighbours form
a bipartite conflict graph; a minimum cut selects the conflict-free subset
with maximum total capped weight. This preserves existing zero-crossing
cells and prevents new ones. The retained transported voxels form
$\Omega_{\rm vol}$ with clipped distances $\Phi_{\rm vol}^+$ and weights
$w_{\rm vol}^+$.

The three sets are thus disjoint, and
$\Omega^+=\Omega_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}\cup\Omega_{\rm vol}$ is the set of
supported output voxels:
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^+,\widetilde w^+)=\begin{cases}
(s_{\rm tr}d^+,\,w_{\rm src}^++w_{\rm add}^+), & x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
(s_f^+d^+,\,w_{\rm fb}+w_{\rm add}^+), & x\in\Omega_{\rm fb},\\
(\Phi_{\rm vol}^+,\,w_{\rm vol}^+), & x\in\Omega_{\rm vol}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_tsdf}
\end{equation}
The final weight is $w^+(x)=\min(w_{\max},\widetilde w^+(x))$.
The result is a native Voxblox TSDF that replaces the active map; fusion
continues on it, and the next correction is handled in the same way.


\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

Let $V_q$ be the number of queried vertices, $Q$ that of examined
vertex--keyframe pairs, $K_{\rm KF}$ that of stored keyframes with $|D|$
samples each, $A$ that of Marching-Cubes cells that emit triangles of
$\mathcal M^-$, $P$ the number of vertices in the deformation solve, and $X^-,X^+$ the source and output voxel
slots. Stages~1--2, 3, and 4 cost
$C_{\rm corr}=C_{\rm assoc}+C_{\rm def}(A,P)+C_{\rm vol}(X^-,X^+)$.
Reading every keyframe and rebuilding the index gives
$C_{\rm assoc}=O(K_{\rm KF}|D|+(K_{\rm KF}+V_q)\log K_{\rm KF}+Q)$ for RGB-D
and $O(K_{\rm KF}|D|+V_q+Q)$ for LiDAR. $C_{\rm def}$ covers proxy
construction, target consolidation, ARAP, transfer, and repair;
$C_{\rm vol}$ covers surface-local reconstruction, observed-volume
transport, and sign-conflict selection. $P$ is the actual proxy size, or
$|V^-|$ on the direct path.
Reintegrating $N$ frames costs $\Theta(N|D|)$. WarpVox uses keyframe samples
to construct targets and updates the existing map through surface deformation
and volume reconstruction.
