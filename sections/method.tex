\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
Following the local-consistency assumption of
Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, we assume that TSDF fusion under the
drifted pre-correction trajectory remains locally valid: surface geometry and
connectivity are coherent in local neighborhoods although the map is globally
inconsistent. WarpVox corrects this global inconsistency after loop closure;
severely noisy or topologically corrupted source maps are outside its scope.

\subsection{Problem and Correction Inputs}

Let $T^-(t),T^+(t)\in SE(3)$ map the sensor frame at time $t$ to the world
frame before and after trajectory correction. The map built with $T^-$ consists
of a TSDF $\Phi^-$, its weight field $w^-$, voxel size $s$, and truncation
distance $\tau$. WarpVox begins its correction after receiving the source mesh $\mathcal M^-=(V^-,F)$ extracted from the active Voxblox TSDF by Marching Cubes (MC)~\cite{10.1145/37402.37422}. Let $\mathcal A$ denote the active TSDF cells that contribute triangles to $\mathcal M^-$. During online fusion, the implementation stores the timestamped, modality-native range images of SLAM keyframes so that these measurements remain available at later correction events,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\rm KF}=\{k_i=(t_i,D_i):i\in{\rm KF}\},
\label{eq:keyframe_cache}
\end{equation}
where ${\rm KF}$ indexes the retained SLAM keyframes and $D_i$ is an RGB-D depth image or a spherical LiDAR range image. Together with $T^-$, these measurements form the observation input to Stage~1. The measurements remain available across correction events, whereas event-specific vertex records, targets, and deformation state are discarded after the corrected volume is committed. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the four stages.

\subsection{Stage 1: Keyframe--Surface Association}

Stage~1 projects the current surface into the keyframe range images and returns vertex-indexed observation records,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O&=\mathcal R(\mathcal M^-,T^-,\mathcal C_{\rm KF}),\\
\mathcal O_j&=\{(r,\alpha_{rj})\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:resolved_records}
\end{equation}
where each record $r$ identifies a keyframe range image and a valid projected range sample, $t_r$ is its acquisition time, and $\alpha_{rj}\in[0,1]$ is the
effective reliability of associating it with source vertex $v_j^-$. After exact association, a fixed maximum number of observations is retained per vertex for target construction. RGB-D conservatively restricts candidates with a camera-centre KD-tree and a bounding sphere of the finite camera frustum, whereas LiDAR uses a sensor-position grid with cached local candidates followed by an exact 80\,m radius check; both then apply the unchanged projection and association tests. Records are matched by acquisition time rather than trajectory row index. The correction
backend therefore receives vertex-indexed records and never re-fuses
measurements into the volume. Its contract is
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^-,w^-,\mathcal M^-,T^+,\mathcal O)
\longmapsto (\Phi^+,w^+),
\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
where the warped mesh is intermediate and $(\Phi^+,w^+)$ is a
Voxblox-compatible TSDF volume.

For an RGB-D record $r$, pixel $\bar u_r=[u_r,v_r,1]^\top$ with measured
z-depth $z_r$ defines the invariant sensor-frame sample
\begin{equation}
m_r=z_rK^{-1}\bar u_r,
\qquad
\widetilde m_r=[m_r^\top,1]^\top.
\label{eq:sensor_sample}
\end{equation}
Let $\rho_r=z_r$ for RGB-D and $\rho_r=\|m_r\|$ for LiDAR. The inverse
pre-correction pose projects source vertex $v_j^-$ into the record frame.
Candidate observations must agree in depth, three-dimensional position, and
viewing direction. Let $n_j^-$ be the source normal, $\ell_{rj}^-$ the unit
viewing direction in the pre-correction world frame, and $z_{rj}^{\rm proj}$
the projected vertex depth. We define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e^g_{rj}
&=\left\|[T^-(t_r)\widetilde m_r]_{1:3}-v_j^-\right\|,\\
e^z_{rj}
&=z_{rj}^{\rm proj}-z_r,\\
g_{rj}
&=\left|(n_j^-)^\top \ell_{rj}^-\right|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:association_residuals}
\end{equation}
The effective association reliability is
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{rj}=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{e^g_{rj}}{\sigma_g}\right)
\exp\!\left(-\frac{|e^z_{rj}|}{\sigma_z}\right)
\frac{g_{rj}}{1+z_{rj}^{\rm proj}/z_0}.
\label{eq:association_weight}
\end{equation}
Candidates satisfy the range-dependent geometric gate
$e^g_{rj}\le\epsilon_g(\rho_r)$. Depth residual and viewing direction enter
$\alpha_{rj}$, and candidates with $\alpha_{rj}<\alpha_{\min}$ are rejected. LiDAR uses the analogous construction with
spherical projection and radial range. This is geometric support rather than
exact voxel-integration provenance. The $z_0$ term provides distance-aware
ranking during association; a separate range factor below models target
reliability after association.

\subsection{Stage 2: Corrected Surface Targets}

After loop closure, the timestamp $t_r$ selects the corresponding corrected
pose. Missing interior poses use linear translation interpolation and SLERP
rotation over a bounded gap~\cite{shoemake1985slerp}. The invariant sample then
defines the corrected candidate
\begin{equation}
q_{rj}^+=\left[T^+(t_r)\widetilde m_r\right]_{1:3}.
\label{eq:corrected_target}
\end{equation}
This candidate is exact when the static-scene association, calibration, and
corrected pose are valid. Let
$\mathcal J_j=\{r:(r,\alpha_{rj})\in\mathcal O_j\}$. We first compute
\begin{equation}
\omega_{rj}=\frac{\alpha_{rj}}{1+\rho_r/\rho_t},
\qquad
\bar q_j=
\frac{\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\omega_{rj}q_{rj}^+}
     {\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\omega_{rj}}.
\label{eq:target_initial}
\end{equation}
The reliability-weighted center is paired with an unweighted median residual
scale so that no single high-weight candidate controls both the estimate and
its rejection threshold:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_j&=\max\!\left(
\epsilon_t,
\kappa_t\operatorname*{median}_{r\in\mathcal J_j}
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|\right),\\
I_j&=\left\{r\in\mathcal J_j:
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|\le\theta_j\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:target_inliers}
\end{equation}
When fewer than three candidates are available, $I_j=\mathcal J_j$. The final
target and its observable dispersion are
\begin{equation}
v_j^*=
\frac{\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}q_{rj}^+}
     {\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}},
\qquad
\sigma_j^2=
\frac{\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}\|q_{rj}^+-v_j^*\|^2}
     {\sum_{r\in I_j}\omega_{rj}}.
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
A target becomes a control only if $|I_j|\ge n_{\min}$ and
$\sigma_j\le\sigma_{\max}$. Dispersion measures agreement among retained
candidates but cannot detect a mutually consistent yet incorrect association.

\subsection{Stage 3: Event-Level Surface Deformation}

The deformation stage propagates observation-derived motion; it does not infer
correction from shape alone. Let $d_j=v_j^*-v_j^-$ be an accepted control
displacement. Before proxy construction, an inverse-distance prediction from
neighbouring controls in the same component rejects displacements whose
residual exceeds the component median by $\kappa_d$ times its MAD.

Component-separated spatial clustering~\cite{rossignac1993vertexclustering}
constructs a triangular proxy $\mathcal P^-=(P^-,F_P)$ and selects one QEM
representative~\cite{garland1997qem} per occupied cell. Let
$\pi:V^-\rightarrow P^-$ map source vertices to representatives and
$\mathcal K_a=\{j:\pi(j)=a\}$ collect their controls. For
$\mathcal K_a\neq\emptyset$, the measured proxy target is
$p_a^{(0)}=p_a^-+|\mathcal K_a|^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal K_a}d_j$.
A local-rigid consensus validates measured targets and supplies motion to
unsupported proxy regions. Welsch-weighted orthogonal-Procrustes
fits~\cite{schoenemann1966procrustes,yao2020welsch} estimate local motion; a
measured target is replaced only when its compatibility residual lies outside
the local median--MAD envelope and more than half of its same-component support
agrees with the fitted motion. Components without accepted controls inherit
robust nearby motion through three spatially separated synthetic anchors. Let
$\mathcal C_P\subseteq\{1,\ldots,|P^-|\}$ index the proxy vertices with accepted
measured or synthesized targets $p_a^*$.

WarpVox performs a single event-level proxy ARAP
optimization~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007,
10.1007/978-3-662-05105-4_2,libigl}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{P^+,\{R_a\in SO(3)\}}\quad
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{|P^-|}
\sum_{b\in\mathcal N_P(a)}c_{ab}\\[-1mm]
&\left\|(p_a^+-p_b^+)-R_a(p_a^--p_b^-)\right\|^2,\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&p_a^+=p_a^*,\qquad a\in\mathcal C_P .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:arap}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal N_P(a)$ is the one-ring neighbourhood induced by $F_P$, and
$c_{ab}=c_{ba}$ are symmetric cotangent-derived weights; the factor $1/2$
removes double counting. Association reliability and robust aggregation decide
which controls survive and where they lie. Once accepted, controls are imposed
exactly so that ARAP does not attenuate the supplied trajectory correction.
The standard local--global iterations solve this single event-level
optimization.

Proxy displacement is transferred to $V^-$ by topology-local barycentric
interpolation. Let $f_j$ be the closest triangle to $v_j^-$ in
$\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$. With barycentric coordinates $\lambda_{jb}$ of
its closest point,
\begin{equation}
v_j^+=v_j^-+\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}(p_b^+-p_b^-),
\qquad \lambda_{jb}\ge0,\quad\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}=1.
\label{eq:proxy_transfer}
\end{equation}
Risk-region displacements are harmonically smoothed with fixed boundary conditions; faces that remain severely distorted under fixed edge-stretch or area-ratio criteria are then removed, while aspect ratio only triggers repair. The retained surfaces satisfy
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal M}^-=(\bar V^-,\bar F),\qquad
\mathcal M^+=(\bar V^+,\bar F),\qquad \bar F\subseteq F.
\label{eq:retained_topology}
\end{equation}
Deformation accuracy therefore depends on target correctness and coverage.

\subsection{Stage 4: Source-Supported TSDF Reconstruction}

The warped surface supplies the corrected zero set~\cite{sussman1999redistancing},
while the source TSDF primarily supplies observed-space semantics. Let $n_b$ be the
number of voxels per block edge, $h=n_bs$ the block width, and $o_b=hb$ and
$c_b=o_b+\frac{h}{2}\mathbf 1$ the origin and centre of block $b$. With
$\mathcal N_1$ denoting a one-block Chebyshev dilation, the allocation domain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_V^+&=\{\lfloor \bar v/h\rfloor:\bar v\in\bar V^+\},\\
\mathcal B^+&=\{b\in\mathcal N_1(\mathcal B_V^+):\\[-1mm]
&\hspace{13mm}\operatorname{dist}(c_b,\mathcal M^+)
 \le\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}h+\tau\},\\
\mathcal X^+&=\{o_b+s(k+\tfrac12\mathbf 1):b\in\mathcal B^+,\\[-1mm]
&\hspace{25mm}k\in\{0,\ldots,n_b-1\}^3\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
No scene-wide bounding box is voxelized:
$|\mathcal X^+|=n_b^3|\mathcal B^+|
\le27n_b^3|\mathcal B_V^+|$ follows the block footprint of the warped surface.
For $x\in\mathcal X^+$, a closest-triangle
query~\cite{10.1145/2601097.2601199} on
$\mathcal M^+$ returns face $f$, closest point $q^+$, and barycentric
coordinates $\beta$. Shared face indices locate the corresponding source point
\begin{equation}
q^-=\sum_{k\in f}\beta_k\bar v_k^-.
\label{eq:source_correspondence}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
R_f^\pm=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_{f,1}^\pm & t_{f,2}^\pm & n_f^\pm
\end{bmatrix}\in SO(3)
\label{eq:triangle_frames}
\end{equation}
be consistently oriented orthonormal frames of the corresponding source and
warped triangles. The first tangent follows the same indexed edge and
$t_{f,2}^\pm=n_f^\pm\times t_{f,1}^\pm$. A piecewise local-rigid pullback,
motivated by deformation transfer~\cite{sumner2004deformationtransfer}, maps
$x$ to the source volume:
\begin{equation}
\Psi_f(x)=q^-+R_f^-(R_f^+)^\top(x-q^+),
\qquad x^-=\Psi_f(x).
\label{eq:inverse_map}
\end{equation}
This preserves tangential and normal offsets relative to the corresponding
triangles. With trilinear source samples $\widetilde\Phi^-$ and
$\widetilde w^-$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d^+(x)&=\min\!\left(\tau,\|x-q^+\|\right),\\
\widehat{\Omega}_{\rm tr}&=
\left\{x\in\mathcal X^+:\widetilde w^-(\Psi_f(x))>0\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:transferred_support}
\end{equation}
Let
\[
\Omega_{\rm nb}=
\left\{x\in\widehat{\Omega}_{\rm tr}:
\left|\widetilde\Phi^-(\Psi_f(x))\right|<\tau\right\}
\]
be the transferred source narrow band. Each 6-connected component of
$\widehat{\Omega}_{\rm tr}\setminus\Omega_{\rm nb}$ is retained only if it
touches $\Omega_{\rm nb}$; an extended voxel is rejected if its insertion
would create a sign change across an adjacent active-voxel pair. Let
$\Omega_{\rm tr}$ denote $\Omega_{\rm nb}$ together with the surviving
transferred extension.

Let $\partial_{\rm open}\mathcal M^+$ denote the union of open boundary edges
and their incident vertices. Fallback support is restricted to unsupported
voxels with strictly positive finite-band weight:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\rm fb}=
\left\{x\in\mathcal X^+\setminus\Omega_{\rm tr}:
\|x-q^+\|<1.5s,\ q^+\notin\partial_{\rm open}\mathcal M^+\right\}.
\label{eq:fallback_support}
\end{equation}
Its weight is
\begin{equation}
w_{\rm fb}(x)=\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(
1.5-\frac{\|x-q^+\|}{s}\right).
\label{eq:fallback_weight}
\end{equation}
The warped normal is oriented to preserve the source TSDF sign convention; let
$s_f^+(x)=\operatorname{sgn}((x-q^+)^\top n_f^+)$. Define
$\Omega^+=\Omega_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}$ and
$\Phi^+:\Omega^+\rightarrow[-\tau,\tau]$ by
\begin{equation}
\Phi^+(x)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\widetilde\Phi^-(\Psi_f(x))\right)d^+(x),
& x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
s_f^+(x)d^+(x),
& x\in\Omega_{\rm fb}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_tsdf}
\end{equation}
The output weight is
\begin{equation}
w^+(x)=
\begin{cases}
\widetilde w^-(\Psi_f(x)), & x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
w_{\rm fb}(x), & x\in\Omega_{\rm fb},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_weight}
\end{equation}
Because transferred weights are positive on $\Omega_{\rm tr}$ and the fallback
band is strict, by construction
$\Omega^+=\{x\in\mathcal X^+:w^+(x)>0\}$. Outside $\Omega^+$ the zero weight
makes the stored distance immaterial. When no source TSDF is available, the final Stage-3 surface is converted into a closest-triangle-normal signed Euclidean distance field within 1.5 voxel widths, with synthetic finite-band weights. The resulting
$(\Phi^+,w^+)$ is serialized in the native Voxblox TSDF format; its MC mesh is used for surface evaluation and visualization.

\subsection{Complexity and Scope}

Let $A=|\mathcal A|$, $K_{\rm KF}=|\mathcal C_{\rm KF}|$, $P=|P^-|$, and $X^+=|\mathcal X^+|$ be the numbers of active source cells, stored keyframe range images, proxy vertices, and allocated output voxels. At fixed resolution, MC produces only a constant number of surface elements per active cell, so $|V^-|=O(A)$. The stored measurements and fixed per-vertex association state require $O(\sum_{i\in{\rm KF}}|D_i|)$ and $O(A)$ storage, respectively.

Let $\kappa\le K_{\rm KF}$ be the maximum number of keyframes returned for one source vertex by the modality-specific spatial index. Under the standard output-sensitive spatial-index model, RGB-D association costs $O(K_{\rm KF}\log K_{\rm KF}+A(\log K_{\rm KF}+\kappa))$; with fixed grid resolution and search radius, LiDAR association costs $O(K_{\rm KF}+A(1+\kappa))$. Denoting the corresponding indexed association cost by $C_{\rm assoc}$, the complete correction cost is
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm corr}=C_{\rm assoc}+C_{\rm ARAP}(P,F_P)
+C_{\rm vol}(X^+,\bar F),
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\rm vol}$ is the accelerated closest-triangle reconstruction cost. The block-local allocation in \eqref{eq:allocation} makes $X^+$ follow the warped-surface footprint rather than scene extent. In the worst case $\kappa=K_{\rm KF}$, so the spatial indices exploit locality without changing the association rule or its worst-case dependence on surface size and keyframe count. The reported implementation uses a 60K-vertex proxy cap; all remaining thresholds and discrete predicates are fixed within each sensor modality. Full reintegration incurs $O(N|\Omega|)$ historical volumetric replay. Measurement storage grows linearly with $K_{\rm KF}$.
