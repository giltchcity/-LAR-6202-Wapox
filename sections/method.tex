\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
Following the local-consistency assumption of
Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph}, we assume that the pre-correction
TSDF remains locally valid: surface geometry and connectivity remain coherent
in local neighbourhoods despite global inconsistency. Severely noisy or
topologically corrupted source maps are outside our scope.

\subsection{Problem and Correction Inputs} Let $T^-(t),T^+(t)\in SE(3)$ map the sensor frame at time $t$ to the world frame before and after trajectory correction. The map built with $T^-$ consists of a TSDF $\Phi^-$, its weight field $w^-$, voxel size $s$, and truncation distance $\tau$. During online fusion, Voxblox continuously maintains the Marching-Cubes surface $\mathcal M^-=(V^-,F)$ of the active TSDF~\cite{10.1145/37402.37422}. On a trajectory correction, this mesh becomes the source surface. Let $\mathcal A$ denote the active TSDF cells that contribute triangles to $\mathcal M^-$. WarpVox stores the timestamped, modality-native range images of SLAM keyframes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\rm KF}=\{k_i=(t_i,D_i):i\in{\rm KF}\},
\end{equation}
where ${\rm KF}$ indexes the retained SLAM keyframes and $D_i$ is an RGB-D depth image or a spherical LiDAR range image. Together with $T^-$, these measurements form the observation input to Stage~1. The measurements remain available across correction events. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the four stages.

The observation records and warped mesh are intermediate; WarpVox returns
a native Voxblox volume $(\Phi^+,w^+)$.

\subsection{Stage 1: Correspondence Establishment}

Stage~1 establishes projective correspondences between the current source
surface and the keyframe range measurements under the pre-correction
trajectory. For each queried source vertex $v_j^-$ with normal $n_j^-$, a
spatial index over sensor positions retrieves candidate keyframes that may
observe it.


Let $r$ index a candidate measurement with timestamp $t_r$ and range image
$D_r$. The source vertex is transformed into the corresponding sensor frame
and sampled in the measurement domain:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat v_{rj}^-
&=
\left[
[T^-(t_r)]^{-1}\widetilde v_j^-
\right]_{1:3},\\
m_{rj}
&=
\mathcal S_m\!\left(
D_r,\Pi_m(\hat v_{rj}^-)
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sensor_sample}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi_m$ is the pinhole projection for RGB-D or the spherical projection
for LiDAR. For RGB-D, $\mathcal S_m$ samples the projected depth and
back-projects it to the sensor frame. For LiDAR, it selects the closest valid
return in a local neighbourhood of the projected spherical-image location.

Association quality combines three-dimensional and projective-depth
discrepancies for RGB-D, and radial-range and three-dimensional consistency
for LiDAR. Viewing incidence is evaluated in the pre-correction world frame
from $n_j^-$ and the sensor-to-vertex direction.

These cues determine whether a candidate is retained and, when retained,
its reliability $\alpha_{rj}$. The verified tuples
$(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})$ form $\mathcal O_j$, and
$\mathcal O=\{\mathcal O_j\}_j$ is passed to Stage~2.

\subsection{Stage 2: Corrected Surface Targets}

The sensor-frame sample $m_{rj}$ is unchanged by a later revision of the
world-frame trajectory. Let $\widehat T_r^+$ denote the corrected pose
associated with record $r$. RGB-D records are resolved by timestamp, while LiDAR records use
the corresponding scan pose. The corrected candidate is
\begin{equation}
q_{rj}^+
=
\left[
\widehat T_r^+\widetilde m_{rj}
\right]_{1:3}.
\label{eq:corrected_target}
\end{equation}

Let
$\mathcal J_j=\{r:(t_r,m_{rj},\alpha_{rj})\in\mathcal O_j\}$.
The Stage~1 reliability gives the initial target estimate
\begin{equation}
\bar q_j
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in\mathcal J_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target_initial}
\end{equation}

A median-relative residual threshold selects a consistent subset:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_j
&=
\max\!\left(
\epsilon_m,\,
\kappa\operatorname*{median}_{r\in\mathcal J_j}
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|
\right),\\
I_j
&=
\left\{
r\in\mathcal J_j:
\|q_{rj}^+-\bar q_j\|\le\theta_j
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:target_inliers}
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon_m$ reflects the modality's sampling resolution and $\kappa$
is shared across modalities. When fewer than three candidates are available,
no trimming is applied. The final observation-derived target is
\begin{equation}
v_j^*
=
\frac{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}q_{rj}^+
}{
\sum_{r\in I_j}\alpha_{rj}
}.
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

Each supported source vertex yields one target displacement
$v_j^*-v_j^-$ for Stage~3.




\subsection{Stage 3: Surface Deformation}

Stage~3 consolidates the sparse targets component-wise, propagates them
through ARAP, and outputs paired source and warped surfaces for Stage~4.

Let $d_j=v_j^*-v_j^-$ denote a control displacement. A component-restricted
inverse-distance predictor and median -- MAD residual test remove sparse
displacement outliers without mixing disconnected surfaces. Small components
supported only by rejected controls are omitted. Retained components without
a surviving measured control receive up to three spatially separated synthetic
constraints carrying robust motion from nearby accepted controls, removing
the gauge freedom of disconnected ARAP blocks.

For large source meshes, component-aware spatial
clustering~\cite{rossignac1993vertexclustering} constructs a triangular
proxy $\mathcal P^-=(P^-,F_P)$ of at most
$P_{\max}\approx60\,\mathrm{K}$ vertices.
One QEM representative~\cite{garland1997qem} is selected per occupied cell,
while different connected components remain separate during clustering.
Let $\pi:V^-\rightarrow P^-$ map each source vertex to its proxy
representative. Controls mapped to proxy vertex $a$ are combined as
\begin{equation}
p_a^{(0)}
=
p_a^-
+
\frac{1}{|\mathcal K_a|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal K_a}d_j,
\qquad
\mathcal K_a=\{j:\pi(j)=a\}.
\label{eq:proxy_targets}
\end{equation}

The proxy targets are further consolidated by a Welsch-reweighted local
rigid fit~\cite{schoenemann1966procrustes,yao2020welsch}. A measured target
is replaced by its rigid prediction only if it is a gross residual and such
residuals form a strict majority among measured targets in its component
with valid predictions. Synthetic targets are regularized by the same field;
let $\mathcal C_P$ index the resulting targets $p_a^*$.

WarpVox then propagates the consolidated targets with
ARAP~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007}. Define
$\Delta p_{ab}^{\pm}=p_a^{\pm}-p_b^{\pm}$. The deformation is obtained from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{P^+,\{R_a\}}\quad
&\frac{1}{2}
\sum_{a=1}^{|P^-|}
\sum_{b\in\mathcal N_P(a)}
c_{ab}\\[-1mm]
&\qquad\cdot
\left\|
\Delta p_{ab}^+
-
R_a\Delta p_{ab}^-
\right\|^2,\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&R_a\in SO(3),\\
&p_a^+=p_a^*,
\qquad a\in\mathcal C_P .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:arap}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal N_P(a)$ is the proxy one-ring and $c_{ab}$ are symmetric
cotangent-derived weights~\cite{10.1007/978-3-662-05105-4_2}. The system is
solved with libigl~\cite{libigl}. The standard configuration uses the proxy;
the full-resolution ablation applies the same objective directly to the
source mesh.

Proxy displacements are transferred to the source mesh by topology-local
barycentric interpolation. Let $f_j$ be the closest proxy triangle to
$v_j^-$ within $\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$. With barycentric coordinates
$\lambda_{jb}$ of its closest point,
\begin{equation}
v_j^+
=
v_j^-
+
\sum_{b\in f_j}
\lambda_{jb}(p_b^+-p_b^-),
\qquad
\lambda_{jb}\geq0,
\quad
\sum_{b\in f_j}\lambda_{jb}=1.
\label{eq:proxy_transfer}
\end{equation}
Restricting the interpolation to $\operatorname{star}(\pi(j))$ prevents
motion transfer between spatially close but disconnected surfaces.

Faces violating edge-stretch, area-change, or aspect-ratio bounds define a
repair region. Incident displacements are relaxed toward mesh one-ring
means with valid boundary displacements fixed; faces still violating
edge-stretch or area-change bounds are removed from both surfaces. No new
connections are introduced, so
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal M}^-=(\bar V^-,\bar F),
\qquad
\mathcal M^+=(\bar V^+,\bar F),
\qquad
\bar F\subseteq F.
\label{eq:paired_surfaces}
\end{equation}




\subsection{Stage 4: Source-Supported TSDF Reconstruction}

Stage~4 reconstructs a native TSDF from the paired source and warped
surfaces produced by Stage~3. The warped surface defines the corrected zero
set, while the source TSDF provides volumetric state that a surface alone
does not encode: observed support, sign orientation, integration weights,
and free space. Distance magnitude is recomputed from the warped surface,
whereas source state is transferred through the paired surface
correspondence.

Let $(\Phi^-,w^-)$ denote the source TSDF. The corrected volume uses the
same voxel size $s$ and truncation distance $\tau$ as the source volume.
We write $\rho=3s/2$ for the near-surface margin. WarpVox first allocates
the blocks containing warped-surface vertices together with one layer of
neighbouring blocks. Surface-local reconstruction is restricted to this
neighbourhood, while transported free space may subsequently allocate
additional blocks.

For each voxel $x$ in the surface-local domain, a closest-triangle
query~\cite{10.1145/2601097.2601199} on $\mathcal M^+$ returns face $f$,
closest point $q^+$, and barycentric coordinates $\beta$. The shared face
indices give the corresponding source point
$q^-=\sum_{k\in f}\beta_k\bar v_k^-$.

Let $R_f^\pm\in SO(3)$ be paired local frames built from the same indexed
triangle edge and corresponding source and warped mesh normals. Their
relative rotation defines the local-rigid pullback
\begin{equation}
\Psi_f(x)
=
q^-+R_f^-(R_f^+)^\top(x-q^+)
\label{eq:inverse_map}
\end{equation}
Absolute TSDF sign orientation is determined separately from the source
field. This map sends an output voxel to its corresponding source
neighbourhood while preserving tangential and normal offsets relative to
the paired triangles~\cite{sumner2004deformationtransfer}. Let
\begin{equation}
x^-=\Psi_f(x),
\quad
r(x)=\|x-q^+\|,
\quad
d^+(x)=\min(\tau,r(x)).
\label{eq:source_query}
\end{equation}
A trilinear source sample is valid when all contributing voxels have
positive weight, in which case its interpolated weight is transferred.
Otherwise, a positive containing-voxel weight is used without its distance
value; unsupported voxels enter the fallback branch. Denote the transferred
weight by $w_{\rm src}^+(x)$.

The warped mesh normal induces the geometric sign
$s_f^+(x)=\operatorname{sgn}((x-q^+)^\top n_f^+)$. When source TSDF
samples are available on both sides of the paired source face, their
central difference determines whether this sign is retained or flipped.
Otherwise, a consistent source sample is used when available, with
$s_f^+(x)$ as the final fallback. Denote the result by $s_{\rm tr}(x)$.

Valid source samples with $r(x)\leq\rho$ form narrow transferred support;
those with $\rho<r(x)\leq\tau$ form candidate extended support. Both receive
\begin{equation}
\bigl(
\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),
w_{\rm tr}^+(x)
\bigr)
=
\bigl(
s_{\rm tr}(x)d^+(x),
w_{\rm src}^+(x)
\bigr).
\label{eq:transferred_tsdf}
\end{equation}

Where transferred source support is unavailable, voxels satisfying
$r(x)\leq\rho$ receive a finite surface prior, unless their closest surface
feature is an open boundary:
\begin{equation}
w_{\rm fb}(x)
=
\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}
\left(
\frac{\rho-r(x)}{s}
\right).
\label{eq:fallback_weight}
\end{equation}
This prior provides compact support for continued fusion and is not
interpreted as recovered observation count. Its supported voxels form
$\Omega_{\rm fb}$.

Candidate extended support is filtered on the output voxel grid. Using
6-neighbour adjacency, a candidate with an opposite-sign positive-weight
neighbour is removed. A flood fill over the remaining candidates starts from
those touching narrow transferred or fallback support and ends when no new
candidate is reached. The narrow samples and reached candidates form
$\Omega_{\rm tr}$.

Observed source voxels satisfying
$w^-(y)>0$ and $\Phi^-(y)\geq\rho$ are eligible for forward transport.
Within each source TSDF block, they are binned into axis-aligned cells of
$\operatorname{round}(\tau/s)$ voxels per side, clipped to the block extent;
the mean voxel-centre position represents each nonempty group. Group
membership is purely spatial and does not require voxel connectivity.

For group $c$, the closest valid source face to its representative supplies
$q_c^-$ and barycentric coordinates; the same face index and coordinates
supply $q_c^+$ on the warped surface. The corresponding triangle-frame
construction gives $R_c^\pm$. A group is discarded when no admissible face
match is found or either paired frame is degenerate. The forward map is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_c(y)
=
q_c^+
+
R_c^+(R_c^-)^\top(y-q_c^-).
\label{eq:forward_free_space}
\end{equation}
Each $\Gamma_c(y)$ is assigned to its containing output voxel. Insertion is
fill-only: a target with positive weight is left unchanged, so collisions
retain the first accepted sample. Before insertion, a target in the
26-neighbourhood of retained negative support is rejected. Accepted samples
retain positive source-derived values and weights and form
$\Omega_{\rm fs}$, with $(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+,w_{\rm fs}^+)$.

Because fallback applies only without transferred support and free-space
transport writes only unsupported voxels, $\Omega_{\rm tr}$,
$\Omega_{\rm fb}$, and $\Omega_{\rm fs}$ are disjoint. With
$\Omega^+=\Omega_{\rm tr}\cup\Omega_{\rm fb}\cup\Omega_{\rm fs}$, the
corrected field is
\begin{equation}
(\Phi^+(x),w^+(x))
=
\begin{cases}
(\Phi_{\rm tr}^+(x),w_{\rm tr}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm tr},\\
(s_f^+(x)d^+(x),w_{\rm fb}(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fb},\\
(\Phi_{\rm fs}^+(x),w_{\rm fs}^+(x)),
&x\in\Omega_{\rm fs}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_tsdf}
\end{equation}
Voxels outside $\Omega^+$ have zero weight. When the source TSDF is
unavailable, reconstruction reduces to the finite surface prior.
The resulting native Voxblox volume replaces the active map, allowing
subsequent fusion and later corrections to operate directly on the updated
TSDF.


\subsection{Complexity and Scope}

Let $Q$ be the number of surface -- keyframe candidate projections,
$A=|\mathcal A|$ the number of active source cells, $P=|P^-|$ the proxy
size, and $X^-$ and $X^+$ the source and output voxel slots processed in
Stage~4. The per-correction cost is
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm corr}
=
O(K_{\rm KF}+Q)
+
C_{\rm def}(A,P)
+
C_{\rm vol}(X^-,X^+).
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
With $\bar k$ the mean number of candidate keyframes examined per queried
vertex, fixed resolution gives $Q=O(A\bar k)$, with worst case
$O(AK_{\rm KF})$; by construction, $P\leq P_{\max}$.

Full reintegration costs
$C_{\rm reint}=\Theta(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|D_n|)=\Theta(N|D|)$.
When $K_{\rm KF}=\Theta(N)$, the frame-count order is unchanged, but
WarpVox avoids volumetric integration of the $|D|$ range samples in each
historical frame.
