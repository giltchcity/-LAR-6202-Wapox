\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dense 3D maps support navigation, manipulation, and scene understanding in robotics and augmented reality. TSDF methods~\cite{10.1145/3596711.3596726,newcombe2011kinectfusion} integrate range observations incrementally, while sparse volume structures~\cite{10.1145/2508363.2508374,Oleynikova_2017,vizzo2022vdbfusion} make large-scale onboard mapping practical.

These systems accept poses from an external SLAM front-end but generally cannot revise measurements already fused into the map. Loop closure and pose-graph optimization~\cite{Campos_2021,5979949} retroactively correct the trajectory, leaving the TSDF in the old world geometry and therefore inconsistent with the revised poses (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}).

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{The map-rebuild bottleneck.}
The standard remedy is to discard the map and reintegrate all $N$ range frames with corrected poses~\cite{Cramariuc_2023,rosinol2021kimeraslamspatialperception}. Its $O(N|\Omega|)$ cost, for $|\Omega|$ measurements per frame, is paid again after every global correction and can reach minutes on long sessions.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Why existing solutions fall short.}
Submap methods such as Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph} and FlashFusion~\cite{han2018flashfusion} apply rigid per-submap transforms but leave intra-submap drift unresolved. Non-rigid surfel SLAM~\cite{whelan2016elasticfusion} is embedded in its own tracking system rather than exposing an external TSDF-correction interface, while neural implicit maps~\cite{sucar2021imap,zhu2022niceslam,liso2024loopyslam,bruns2024neuralgraphmap} require representation-specific re-optimization.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Observation invariance: the key insight.}
A calibrated range sample expressed in its sensor frame is unchanged by a later
world-frame pose correction, but its association to an evolving map surface is
not. WarpVox therefore caches timestamped keyframe range images and rebuilds
surface associations at every correction event. Re-expressing the matched
samples with corrected poses yields corrected world-space surface targets, so
correction re-queries a sparse keyframe cache rather than re-fusing the dense
range stream.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Our approach.}
WarpVox has four stages: (i)~re-associate the current pre-correction surface
with cached keyframe observations, (ii)~compute robust targets from the
corrected poses, (iii)~propagate sparse displacements through a single
event-level ARAP optimization~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007} on a QEM proxy, and
(iv)~reconstruct a TSDF whose zero set follows the warped surface while
observed support, sign, and weight follow the source volume. The final stage
prevents a surface-only conversion from labeling unsupported space as
observed. The corrected volume replaces the active TSDF and fusion resumes from
the next frame. Only Stage~1 revisits cached keyframes; deformation and
reconstruction follow the active surface and output volume rather than the
accumulated dense stream.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate loop-closure map correction from persistent keyframe range images with event-wise surface reassociation, avoiding volumetric replay of accumulated dense frames.
    \item We combine observation-derived targets, one event-level proxy deformation, and source-supported TSDF reconstruction to return a native Voxblox TSDF suitable for continued fusion.
    \item We evaluate RGB-D and LiDAR correction against reintegration and mapping baselines over 51 checkpoints, compare final-state correction with Kimera-PGMO on ten complete bags, and demonstrate transfer of the target mechanism to Gaussian primitives.
\end{itemize}
