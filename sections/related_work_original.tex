\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Volumetric mapping.}
TSDF fusion~\cite{10.1145/237170.237269,newcombe2011kinectfusion} and sparse volume structures~\cite{10.1145/2508363.2508374,Oleynikova_2017,vizzo2022vdbfusion} enable incremental dense mapping from externally supplied poses. These systems normally assume that the input trajectory remains fixed and do not expose a mechanism for revising a persistent volume after a pose-graph backend changes historical poses. Wavemap~\cite{reijgwart2023wavemap} achieves state-of-the-art memory and computational efficiency through wavelet-compressed multi-resolution occupancy mapping; as an occupancy representation, it likewise rebuilds from measurements after a trajectory revision.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Dense-map correction after loop closure.} Kintinuous~\cite{whelan2015kintinuous} deforms point-cloud slices, while ElasticFusion~\cite{whelan2016elasticfusion} deforms a surfel map within its tracking system. Maier et al.~\cite{BMVC2017_158} correct a voxel-hashed SDF after pose-graph updates by reintegrating fused RGB-D keyframes; WarpVox uses keyframe measurements only to construct deformation targets and performs no volumetric replay. Voxgraph~\cite{reijgwart2020voxgraph} optimizes rigid TSDF submaps, making correction cheap but requiring a partitioned volume and leaving intra-submap drift. Kimera-PGMO~\cite{kimera_pgmo} jointly optimizes poses and a timestamped
mesh, producing only a corrected surface, not a volume.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Surface deformation.}
ARAP~\cite{ARAP_modeling:2007} and embedded deformation~\cite{10.1145/1276377.1276478} preserve local shape while satisfying sparse controls. DynamicFusion~\cite{7298631}, KillingFusion~\cite{slavcheva2017killingfusion}, and VolumeDeform~\cite{innmann2016volumedeform} estimate continuous non-rigid scene motion. WarpVox addresses a static scene with a discrete trajectory revision:
corrected poses provide controls, propagated by a single QEM-proxy
surface deformation.

\vspace{1mm}\noindent\textit{Neural and Gaussian maps.}
Neural SLAM systems~\cite{sucar2021imap,zhu2022niceslam,cui2023ngelslam} optimize implicit scene representations; Loopy-SLAM~\cite{liso2024loopyslam} revisits that optimization after loop closure, while Neural Graph Map~\cite{bruns2024neuralgraphmap} anchors local fields to pose-graph nodes. SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2024splatam} maps with 3D Gaussians~\cite{kerbl2023gaussians}, and LoopSplat~\cite{zhu2025_loopsplat} aligns Gaussian submaps. These representations do not generally provide the explicit volumetric state
required by TSDF pipelines. WarpVox targets such pipelines; its
Gaussian experiment evaluates the transferability of observation-derived
targets.
