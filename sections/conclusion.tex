\section{Conclusion}
WarpVox converts an external trajectory revision into a native Voxblox TSDF by
re-associating cached keyframe range images with the current surface,
re-expressing the matched samples with corrected poses, propagating the
resulting targets through one event-level deformation, and transferring
source-volume support to the corrected zero set. It avoids re-fusing the
accumulated dense range stream: association follows the eligible keyframes and
active surface, while deformation and reconstruction follow active geometry.
Across RGB-D and LiDAR benchmarks, WarpVox remains close to full reintegration
with mean core-time speedups of $43.46\times$ and $3.23\times$, respectively. A
separate ten-bag final-state surface comparison shows that one mesh-only
WarpVox update is competitive with continuous Kimera-PGMO mesh optimization
while PGMO accumulates $9.27\times$ more core time over its callbacks. The
single-sequence Gaussian-center extension indicates that the target
construction is not tied to voxel grids. The returned TSDF preserves the
representation required for continued robotic mapping.
