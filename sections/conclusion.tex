\section{Conclusion}
WarpVox converts an external trajectory revision into a native Voxblox TSDF by re-associating keyframe range images with the current surface, re-expressing the matched samples with corrected poses, propagating the resulting targets through one deformation per correction, and transferring source-derived support, sign, and integration weights into the reconstructed volume around the corrected zero set. It does not re-integrate measurements: correction targets come from projection into cached keyframes, so the cost follows the current map rather than the number of measurements behind it or the volumetric work of fusing them. Across RGB-D and LiDAR benchmarks, WarpVox remains close to full reintegration with mean core-time speedups of $48.43\times$ and $3.23\times$, respectively. A separate ten-bag surface comparison shows that one mesh-only WarpVox update is competitive with Kimera-PGMO while using $15.24\times$ lower core time than the PGMO mesh deformation required to produce its final mesh. The single-sequence Gaussian-center case study suggests that the target construction may transfer beyond voxel grids. The returned TSDF preserves the representation required for continued robotic mapping.
