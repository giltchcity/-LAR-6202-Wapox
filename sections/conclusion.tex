\section{Conclusion}
WarpVox converts an external trajectory revision into a native Voxblox TSDF by re-associating keyframe range images with the current surface, re-expressing the matched samples with corrected poses, propagating the resulting targets through one event-level deformation, and transferring source-volume support to the corrected zero set. It does not re-integrate measurements: keyframe measurements are projectively re-associated with the active surface, while deformation and reconstruction follow active geometry. Across RGB-D and LiDAR benchmarks, WarpVox remains close to full reintegration with mean core-time speedups of $48.43\times$ and $3.23\times$, respectively. A separate ten-bag final-state surface comparison shows that one mesh-only WarpVox update is competitive with Kimera-PGMO while using $15.24\times$ lower core time than the PGMO mesh deformation required to produce its final mesh. The single-sequence Gaussian-center case study suggests that the target construction may transfer beyond voxel grids. The returned TSDF preserves the representation required for continued robotic mapping.
