\begin{abstract}
Loop closure retroactively changes sensor poses after their range observations have been fused, leaving a TSDF inconsistent with the corrected trajectory. Full reintegration restores consistency by replaying the complete measurement history, at a prohibitive runtime cost that grows with the session length and is paid again at every correction. 
% WarpVox reconstructs a native Voxblox TSDF whose zero set follows the corrected surface while its volumetric support, sign, and weights primarily follow the source volume. 
WarpVox instead corrects the map in place: projecting the current surface into keyframes recovers supporting measurements for each surface point; corrected poses re-express these samples as surface targets, a single deformation propagates the targets, and the TSDF is rebuilt around the deformed surface.
The result is a native Voxblox TSDF whose zero set follows the corrected surface while its volumetric support, sign, and weights primarily follow the source volume; it replaces the active map, allowing fusion and subsequent loop-closure corrections to continue from the updated state. 
% Across 39 Kimera-Multi and 12 KITTI checkpoints at 0.25 m, WarpVox remains within 0.05 and 0.32 percentage points of reintegration at F@25 while reducing mean core time from 109.25 to 2.26 s and from 15.73 to 4.87 s. In a ten-bag final-state surface comparison, a single mesh-only WarpVox update improves mean F@50 by 3.03 percentage points over Kimera-PGMO, while using $15.24\times$ lower core time than the PGMO mesh deformation required to produce its final mesh.
Across 51 loop closures on Kimera-Multi and KITTI, WarpVox stays within 0.32 percentage points of full reintegration at F@25 while achieving a $48\times$ mean update-time speedup on the RGB-D sequences. Compared with Kimera-PGMO, a deformation-graph mesh optimization method, WarpVox achieves comparable corrected-surface accuracy at lower cost.
A single-sequence case study extends the same targets to Gaussian centres.
